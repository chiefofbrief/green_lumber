# AppLovin (APP) — Q2 FY2026 Breakdown

## Synthesis and Takeaways

- **The quality is elite and not in question; the growth trajectory is the whole debate.** Margins are roughly 88% gross, 78% operating, and 66% net, free cash flow is about 66% of revenue, and returns on capital run well above 100% (10-Q Q2 2026; FMP). Unlike most software, the profit is genuinely backed by cash, so there is no inflated adjusted number hiding a worse one (FMP; 10-K FY2025).
- **Growth is fast but clearly decelerating, which is the opposite of what stock screens show.** On clean, ads-only numbers the year-over-year rate has slowed every quarter for over a year, from about 77% to about 53% (SEC 10-Qs; FMP). The "acceleration" that flagged this name is a data artifact left over from the 2025 sale of its games business.
- **It is genuinely AI-native, and getting more so.** It trains its own ad-matching models and is now building its own AI video generator for advertisers, which is both a real growth lever and part of what separates it from companies that just bolt AI on (Q2 2026 call).
- **The slowdown may be partly competitive, not just scale.** Its most direct competitor, Unity Vector, accelerated to 23% sequential growth in the same quarter AppLovin stalled at about 4%, and Unity is activating a runtime data advantage AppLovin cannot copy (Unity Q2 FY2026 call). Unity is far smaller and far less profitable, so it is a rising challenger rather than a scale threat, but this is the clearest external warning on AppLovin's growth durability.
- **The biggest risks are structural dependence and unproven expansion.** It lives and dies by Apple and Google, its number one named risk (10-K FY2025), and its next act is a broad, unfocused push into e-commerce with no clear second niche.
- **The stock got much cheaper while earnings rose, and the next print is binary.** It trades near 26 times earnings versus about 56 a year ago and is down roughly 55% from its high (market data). Management blamed the Q2 miss on temporary model timing and says a better model is already live, guiding Q3 to about 47% growth (Q2 2026 call). The roughly Nov 4 report either proves the miss was a blip or proves the slowdown is structural.

Put together, this looks like paying about 75 to 80 cents for a dollar of value. The economics and optionality are elite and the balance sheet is a fortress, so the discount is real, but the growth rate is genuinely uncertain until the next report settles the timing-versus-structural question, and a fast-rising competitor with a data edge is now a live part of that question, so it is not a slam-dunk bargain.

## Growth

The headline is strong in level, weakening in trend, and increasingly AI-native.

AppLovin is AI-native in a meaningful way rather than a superficial one. It trains its own models, the Axon recommendation engine, to match ads to users, and that engine (not more ad inventory) is what produces growth. In Q2, revenue per install rose 58% while the number of installs actually fell 2%, so essentially all growth came from the model getting smarter (10-Q Q2 2026). The company is now extending that same approach into creative by building its own AI video generator so advertisers can produce ads out of the box, which both lowers the barrier for new advertisers and reinforces the AI-native identity (Q2 2026 call).

The problem is the trend. Once you strip out the games business they sold in mid-2025 and compare ads-only to ads-only, the year-over-year growth rate has fallen every single quarter:

- Roughly +77% (Q2 2025), +68% (Q3 2025), +66% (Q4 2025), +59% (Q1 2026), +53% (Q2 2026) (SEC 10-Qs; FMP).

A key correction: the large positive "growth acceleration" that helped this name score highly on screens is a data artifact. The data provider carried a prior-year quarter as the old, larger company with games still in it, faking a low prior growth rate. Recomputed on clean data, the screener growth score drops from about 93 to about 55, driven entirely by the acceleration measures flipping from top-tier to bottom-quartile (recomputed from the screener formula on clean data).

The forward story has two legs. Gaming, which they dominate, is the mature base and the source of the Q2 stumble. Consumer and e-commerce is the newer, faster-growing leg, with advertiser spend running 28% above the prior holiday peak in a seasonally weak quarter (Q2 2026 call). Guidance implies continued year-over-year deceleration even as it reaccelerates sequentially: Q3 is guided to about 47% year-over-year and 7 to 8% sequentially, and management reset the long-term rate to "roughly 30% annually" (Q2 2026 call).

## Profitability

This is the strongest and least disputed part of the story.

- **Margins are elite and stable.** Q2 was about 88% gross, 78% operating, and 66% net, and those levels have held tightly for four straight quarters (10-Q Q2 2026; FMP). Adjusted EBITDA was about $1.61 billion at an 84% margin (10-Q; Q2 2026 call).
- **Free cash flow is enormous.** It runs around 66% of revenue on a trailing basis. Q2 free cash flow of $863 million looked light, but that was tax and interest payment timing, and management expects it to normalize to about 75% of EBITDA for the full year (Q2 2026 call).
- **The profit is real, not accounting flattery.** Cash flow roughly equals reported net income, and stock-based compensation is only about 4% of revenue, unusual for software (FMP; 10-K FY2025).
- **Returns and the balance sheet are exceptional.** Return on invested capital runs well above 100% trailing, debt is about $3.5 billion against roughly $3 billion of cash (net leverage near zero), and all debt could be repaid from under a year of cash flow (FMP; 10-Q; Q2 2026 call).
- Recomputed on clean Q2 data, the screener profitability score is essentially unchanged at about 97.

One honest caveat: these margins are recent. As late as 2022 the combined company was near breakeven, and clean ads-only margins only go back to 2023. The jump came from selling the low-margin games business and from the AI engine scaling (10-K FY2025).

## Risk

The balance sheet is not the risk. The risks are dependence, unproven expansion, competition, and the fragility of the growth engine.

- **Apple and Google are the top risk.** Their app stores control distribution, fees, and how much user data reaches ad networks, and a rule change could directly hurt ad targeting. It is the number one risk in AppLovin's own filing (10-K FY2025).
- **The most direct competitor is accelerating while AppLovin decelerates, though off a much smaller base.** Unity Vector, an AI ad-matching product in the same mobile-game niche, grew 23% sequentially in Q2 and is guiding to keep accelerating, but it is only about a $1 billion business versus AppLovin's roughly $7.7 billion in ads, and AppLovin still added more revenue in absolute dollars this quarter. The more important point is competitive, not the growth rate: Unity is activating a runtime data advantage (engine-level signals from about 3 billion monthly players of Unity-made games) that AppLovin cannot replicate and calls its primary strategic edge, and AppLovin downplayed exactly this on its own call (Unity Q2 FY2026 call; AppLovin Q2 2026 call). It is the clearest sign the slowdown may be partly competitive.
- **The move into e-commerce is unproven and unfocused.** They are leaving a niche they dominate (gaming) for e-commerce, which is enormous and broad rather than a defined second niche. Management has already walked back its early platform-opening hype, admitted a brand-awareness problem in e-commerce, and said small advertisers are hard to serve today (Q2 2026 call). There is no clear evidence yet that a second durable growth engine exists.
- **The AI video generator is hard and expensive.** It is a genuinely difficult, compute-heavy problem, and management admitted the tool is still not ready for a high-quality video out of the box (Q2 2026 call). If it does not work, the plan to onboard small and long-tail advertisers stalls.
- **Growth is lumpy research and development, by their own admission.** They said plainly there is no guarantee of a model improvement every quarter, and a quarter without one simply misses, which is what caused Q2 (Q2 2026 call Q&A).
- **There is almost no forward visibility.** Contracts are cancelable at any time and revenue is booked per ad, so there is no backlog (10-Q notes). You are trusting the model keeps winning.
- **A tax benefit rolls off.** A Singapore incentive worth about $0.80 per share, roughly 6% of earnings, expires in June 2028 unless renewed (10-K FY2025 notes).
- **Sentiment flags.** Insiders sold about $197 million over three months, and the Q2 report was the first guidance miss in years, sending the stock down about 20% (news, 2026-07-30 and 2026-08-06).

## Narrative

AppLovin spent the last year transforming itself, and the market is now doubting it on a single axis. In mid-2025 it sold its mobile games business to Tripledot for $400 million in cash plus about a 20% equity stake, turning itself from a two-part company into a pure advertising platform (10-K FY2025; 10-Q). What remains is an AI-native ad engine, Axon, that matches ads to users at a computing cost of only about 10 cents per dollar of revenue, which is why the AI is already fully monetized rather than a future promise (Q2 2026 call). The company is now trying to widen that engine in two directions at once: into e-commerce as a second demand source, and into AI-generated video creative as a way to bring on advertisers who cannot make their own ads.

Then came the Q2 stumble. For the first time in years the company missed its own guidance, and the stock fell about 20% (news, 2026-08-06). Management was unusually candid, saying the miss was temporary and caused by a smaller-than-normal model improvement during the quarter, with a better model already live and working in Q3 (Q2 2026 call). That explanation is plausible and consistent with how the business works, since growth genuinely is model improvement, but it also confirmed the bear worry that the growth engine is lumpy and hard to predict.

The competitive backdrop sharpened the same week. Unity, the closest peer, reported the opposite story and its stock spiked, with its Vector ad product accelerating to 23% sequential growth and management touting a new runtime data advantage drawn from about 3 billion monthly players of Unity-made games (Unity Q2 FY2026 call). Interestingly, both companies just sold their game-publishing arms to the same buyer, Tripledot, to become focused ad platforms. Unity is still far smaller and far less profitable, so it is a challenger rather than a scale threat, but it is pressing exactly the data edge AppLovin lacks, and AppLovin brushed the question aside on its own call. That makes the competitive axis a real part of the debate rather than an afterthought.

Sentiment is split, and that split is the story. Value-oriented buyers and most analysts see a cheap, elite business, institutions own roughly 42% and several added to positions, and a regulatory overhang lifted when the SEC closed its inquiry with no action (Q2 2026 call; news). On the other side, the sell-side is quietly cooling, with average price targets marked down over the year and two downgrades on the miss, and the long-term growth framing was just reset lower (analyst consensus, 2026-08-06). Insiders selling roughly $197 million adds to the caution.

The whole debate compresses into one event. Around November 4, the Q3 report will either confirm that Q2 was a timing blip, with the already-live model improvement reaccelerating results toward the guided 47%, or confirm that the deceleration is structural and the long-term rate really is drifting toward 30% or lower. Given the elite economics on one side and the unproven expansion, platform dependence, and a rising competitor on the other, that single print is the near-term swing factor for the thesis.
