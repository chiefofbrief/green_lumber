# AI Sub-Sector Priority List

## Finished (11)
1. Networking. Optical beyond the rack, lasers, silicon photonics, DSP chips.
2. Power. Natural gas and its supply chain first, then nuclear, solar, batteries, advanced geothermal, and the utilities that deliver power.
3. Wafers and advanced packaging. Foundries and outside packagers, plus the equipment and materials upstream.
4. Cooling. The shift from air to liquid at the chip, and air cooling for the rest of the facility.
5. Data. Companies that hold task-specific data (vertical software, data providers, platforms and networks) and companies that make data usable (data cleaning, databases and retrieval).
6. Trust. Identity, security, governance, and observability for agents, plus cost controls.
7. Runtime. Cloud providers and agent runtime software, including inference routing.
8. Processors. Accelerators (GPUs, custom silicon, hyperscalers' own chips, the software around them) and CPUs.
9. Racks and servers. System builders around the accelerator.
10. Memory. HBM first, but DRAM and flash are tight as well. Completes the six physical constraints.
11. Tools. APIs and standards such as MCP, integration and gateway vendors, and payment networks building for agents. Completes data, tools, trust, and runtime.

## Remaining (5)
12. Electrical equipment in the data center (transformers, switchgear), scoped out of Power.
13. Applications. Where the value ends up. AI-native applications are private, so candidates come mainly from data and the other digital sub-sectors until they are investable.
14. Physical AI, components and materials. Movement, sensing, onboard compute, and power for machines.
15. Physical AI, machine makers. Existing machine makers adding AI.
16. Data center developers and operators. The framework calls development the swing factor.
