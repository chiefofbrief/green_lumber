# AI Sub-Sector Priority List

Source: the AI framework. Build and run in this order. Add one at a time.

## Done
- Networking: optical beyond the rack, lasers, silicon photonics, DSP chips.

## Build first
1. Power (natural gas and its supply chain). Power limits how many accelerators can run, and gas is the only source that reliably supplies it now. The processing, gathering, and transmission infrastructure is as much the trade as the gas. Geothermal, nuclear, and solar with batteries are further out.
2. Wafers and advanced packaging. Every accelerator needs both. Concentrated at TSMC, with Samsung and Intel as the other foundries and outside packagers (e.g., Amkor, ASE) emerging.
3. Cooling. The shift from air to liquid at the chip: racks and cold plates, coolant distribution units and pumps, and heat rejection.
4. Data. Task-specific data that is not public: software companies that hold organized data as a byproduct (e.g., Tyler, Agilysys) and vendors that clean, organize, and structure enterprise data. The main route to applications for now.

## Next
5. Trust. Identity, security, governance, and observability for agents, plus cost controls.
6. Runtime. Cloud providers and agent runtime software that keep long-running, stateful agents going through failures.

## Later (order not yet decided)
- Processors. Accelerators and CPUs. The bet is on volume more than on who wins.
- Memory. HBM first, but DRAM and flash are tight as well.
- Databases. Memory that persists for agents: vector databases, knowledge graphs, and incumbents adapting existing products.
- Tools. APIs and standards such as MCP, integration and gateway vendors, and payment networks building for agents.
- Applications. Where the value ends up. AI-native applications are private, so candidates come mainly from data and the other digital sub-sectors until they are investable.
- Physical AI, components and materials. Movement, sensing, onboard compute, and power for machines.
- Physical AI, machine makers. Existing machine makers adding AI.
