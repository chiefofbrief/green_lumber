In base, the three memory makers lead because base keeps every tier tight and memory is their whole business: sales up 219.8% to 272.4% over the year, FCF margins of 44.3% to 59.9%, almost no debt and EV/sales of only 4.18 to 6.32, with SK hynix first as the HBM leader at the lowest EV/sales of the two HBM makers (4.28). Teradyne (sales up 74.9% with margins near their lows, EV/sales 7.73) and Lam, the most memory-exposed toolmaker (up 33.4%), complete the base picks, while ASML, KLA and Advantest fall short on EV/sales of 12.98 to 14.47. In pessimistic, the shortage eases and pricing softens, so the makers' margin gaps of 73 to 90 points become the main risk, but SK hynix and Micron still lead on scale ($201B and $133B of sales), debt of 0.13 years of FCF or less and P/Es of 6.61 and 10.36. Applied (margins at their lows, $34.4B of sales, the lowest EV/sales of the large toolmakers at 8.45), Sandisk (debt of 0.01 years of FCF, P/E 6.48) and KLA (margins near their lows, 27.8% FCF margin) complete the pessimistic picks. Lam is hit most directly by memory capex cuts, Teradyne is mid-sized, and the storage names and borderline suppliers depend mostly on businesses outside memory, while small cash-burning suppliers such as Ultra Clean, Ichor and Aehr sit near the bottom in both scenarios.
