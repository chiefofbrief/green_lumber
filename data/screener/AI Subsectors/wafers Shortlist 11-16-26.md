The order is led by the companies that hold or equip the scarce leading-edge capacity and are growing fast with strong cash generation: TSMC (sales +36.1% TTM, +50.9% latest quarter, FCF 30.5% of sales), Lam Research (+33.4%, accelerating 7.7pp) and Applied Materials (+21.4%, 4-quarter acceleration +56.6pp, the cheapest of the large tool makers at 8.45x EV/sales). ASML has the strongest position but trails them on price (13.0x EV/sales) and a free cash flow margin 41.8pp above its five-year low. Onto, Teradyne and Kulicke and Soffa rank high because they combine some of the fastest growth in the list (Teradyne +74.9% TTM, the highest; Kulicke +122.6% and Onto +87.8% in the latest quarter) with real scale or reasonable valuations, though Teradyne's fit is borderline. Aehr's latest quarter grew faster still (+200.9%), but on only $73M of sales at 24.3x EV/sales. The main trade-offs are the outside packagers, ASE and Amkor, which have strong positions but negative free cash flow from heavy capacity spending, and the mature-node foundries and smaller suppliers, which are cheaper but grow slower or burn cash. DuPont ranks last because its sales fell 43.5% after the electronics business was separated into Qnity.
