Memory makers lead because memory is their whole business and sales are compounding (TTM growth of 219.8% to 272.4%, with annual growth accelerating by 174pp to 261pp). HBM, DRAM and NAND are all tight, so SK hynix, Micron and Sandisk take the top three places. The trade-off is durability and price: their gross margins sit 50.1pp to 89.8pp above their own 5-year troughs, which looks like shortage pricing. They look cheap only on earnings (P/E 7.5 to 11.8), and that is at peak margins. On sales they are in the bottom quarter of the universe (EV/sales percentiles of 12.6 to 22.8), so the low P/E is the market pricing the margins to fall, not a bargain. Next come companies with strong growth and a memory link but margins closer to normal: FormFactor (probe cards, fit tier A), Kulicke and Soffa, Silicon Motion, Lam and Teradyne. Netlist is the other fit-A name, but it has a 16.2% gross margin, negative free cash flow and a P/E of 2,880, so it ranks mid-table. Further down are broad equipment and materials names whose memory exposure is partial or indirect, and then names with negative cash flow, sharp slowdowns or extreme valuations.
