The three memory makers lead because they sit where the Memory Upside puts the demand, every tier is tight, and their figures are extraordinary: sales up 220% to 272% over the last year with the latest quarter up 326% to 379%, free cash flow margins of 44% to 60%, almost no debt, and EV/sales of only 4.2 to 6.3 with P/E of 6.5 to 10.4. The main trade-off is that their gross margins are 73 to 90 points above their five-year lows, so part of the profit is shortage pricing that new capacity would eventually erode; the low multiples already price some of that. Equipment suppliers follow, ordered by growth and price: Teradyne (sales up 75% and accelerating, EV/sales 7.7) and Lam (the most memory-exposed toolmaker, up 33% with the latest quarter up 54%) lead that group, while ASML, KLA and Advantest are held back by EV/sales of about 13 to 14.5. Borderline fits such as hard drive makers, storage system vendors and connectivity chips rank lower because less of their growth comes from memory, and small suppliers with negative free cash flow or little growth fill the bottom.
