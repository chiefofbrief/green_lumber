Vertiv leads because it is the most direct liquid-cooling name and its sales are accelerating (TTM +35.3%, latest quarter +61.2%), but it is also the priciest A-tier name (EV/sales 6.8, P/E 42.7), and its free cash flow margin of 23.8% is 28.4 points above its five-year low, so part of the strength may not last. Modine and AAON follow with central cooling products and fast, accelerating growth (TTM +29.5% and +53.5%), but both have thin cash: Modine's FCF is 3.0% of sales with debt at 6.7x FCF, and AAON's is -6.2%. Comfort Systems (+50.7%) and nVent (+48.0%) come next with strong cash flow (FCF 19.5% and 12.1%) and healthy balance sheets, though Comfort Systems installs cooling rather than making it and its FCF margin is 14.3 points above its low, while nVent's margins sit within 0.7 and 2.6 points of their lows. Trane (+10.3%) is the largest A-tier HVAC maker, and its strength (debt 1.1x FCF) holds it above faster-growing but weaker or lower-fit names such as Supermicro, which is the cheapest growth in the file (EV/sales 0.5, P/E 9.7, +77.8%) but burns cash (FCF -17.9%) and dilutes (+11.0%). The bottom is Solstice and Chemours, where the cooling link is thin and debt is above 10x FCF; Chemours' sales are also shrinking (-1.4%).
