### 2026-11-11 | Fortinet Discloses Critical Flaws Enabling Unauthenticated Code Execution
*7 articles in cluster*

Fortinet disclosed two groups of serious vulnerabilities: a FortiClient origin-validation flaw that can let a malicious website trigger code execution on a user’s computer, and flaws in FortiManager, FortiAnalyzer and FortiOS that may allow unauthenticated code or command execution through exposed management services. The FortiClient attack requires a user to visit the attacker-controlled page; administrators can check for vulnerable versions and, on Windows, look for a service listening on TCP port 9191 or a FortiVRS.exe process. **Key Points:**

- The FortiClient flaw does not affect every release: the 8.0 branch and FortiClient Mac 7.2 are reported as unaffected.
- The management-product disclosures include three separately tracked issues: CVE-2026-126170 in the FortiManager/FortiAnalyzer JSON-RPC API; CVE-2026-126261 in FortiManager’s FGFM service, where the flaw occurs before the registration password is checked; and CVE-2026-126262, an OFTP SQL-injection issue affecting systems with FortiAnalyzer features enabled.

### 2026-11-10 | Researchers Uncover GhostFrame, VMware VM-Hijacking Malware
*6 articles in cluster*

GhostFrame is a newly documented post-compromise framework that gives attackers with root access to VMware ESXi hosts centralized control over the hypervisor and its virtual machines. Researchers at Recorded Future’s Insikt Group linked its use to threat group TAG-218, whose activity overlaps with the Settra ransomware operation. The framework’s implant can persist across reboots, weaken host protections, and support actions such as accessing virtual machines, harvesting credentials, and moving through victim networks. Because GhostFrame requires existing root access rather than exploiting a vulnerability to gain entry, the findings underscore the need to secure privileged access to ESXi and monitor hypervisors for suspicious activity.

**Key Points:**
- GhostFrame’s implant, named vmhostd-core, can disguise itself as VMware’s legitimate vmware-hostd service by renaming itself vmhostd, making it harder for administrators to spot.
- The implant repeatedly restores weakened protections: it edits /etc/rc.local.d/local.sh to relaunch itself and disable the ESXi firewall and the execInstalledOnly setting, which blocks unsigned programs. The article says this happens about every five seconds.
- GhostFrame attempts command-and-control connections over six ports—53, 2222, 3389, 4444, 8443 and 9443—at nearly the same time. That connection pattern may offer defenders a clue for detecting the implant.

### 2026-10-06 | FortiBleed compromises 86,000 firewalls, locks out admins
*10 articles in cluster*

The FBI and U.S. Secret Service warn that the FortiBleed credential-harvesting campaign is ongoing, with more than 86,000 Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and VPN gateways reportedly compromised across 194 countries.
