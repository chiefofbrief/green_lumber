# Unity (U) — Q2 FY2026 Breakdown

## Synthesis and Takeaways

- **This is the near-mirror image of AppLovin: the growth trajectory is elite and accelerating, but the current financials are the whole debate.** Unity has never been profitable on a GAAP basis, runs an operating margin around −30%, and earns a negative return on capital, yet it just guided to its first-ever GAAP profit next quarter and is growing its strategic business 38% year-over-year and accelerating (Unity Q2 FY2026 call; 10-Q Q2 2026).
- **Unity gets two bites at the AI apple, and AppLovin only gets one.** Unity both helps developers *build* games (its Create engine) and helps them *get discovered and monetized* (its Grow/Vector ad business). AppLovin only does the ad side. So if AI increases the number of games and interactive media, Unity benefits twice: more creation drives more engine usage, and more games make discovery harder, which drives more advertising (Unity Q2 FY2026 call).
- **The AI ad engine is genuinely inflecting, and the growth looks organic.** Unity Vector grew 23% sequentially in Q2 against management's own 12 to 13% expectation, hit a $1 billion-plus annual run rate two quarters early, and only $3 million of that growth came from absorbing the ad network it is shutting down. So this is real demand, not cannibalized share (Unity Q2 FY2026 call).
- **The CEO's AI story is unusually convincing and partly backed by data.** Matt Bromberg frames Unity as an AI flywheel (more games, more platform usage, more discovery, all fed by runtime data from about 3 billion monthly players), and the early evidence supports it: new sign-ups grew 20% quarter-over-quarter (fastest since 2020), new Made-with-Unity games rose 12%, and 90% of developers already use AI in their workflows (Unity Q1 and Q2 FY2026 calls).
- **The catch is that the stock already believes all of this.** Unity trades near its 52-week high, up about 161% from its 2024 lows, at roughly 85 times trailing owner earnings with no GAAP earnings to anchor it (market data; FMP; 10-Q). The improvement is real; the discount is not.
- **The single biggest quality gap versus AppLovin is stock-based compensation and cash conversion.** Unity's stock comp is about 17% of revenue on a trailing basis (falling to 14% most recently), roughly four times AppLovin's 4%. That halves Unity's headline free cash flow once you charge it properly, leaving true owner earnings near 10% of revenue versus AppLovin's roughly 62% (10-Q Note 8; management confirmed; FMP).
- **The next print is the catalyst and it is binary.** Around early November, the Q3 report either delivers the promised first-ever GAAP profit with strategic revenue up 44 to 47%, validating the turnaround, or it slips and puts a premium-priced, still-lossmaking stock at risk (Unity Q2 FY2026 call).

Put together, this looks like paying about a dollar for a dollar of value, maybe 90 cents to 110 cents depending on the Q3 print. The business quality is improving fast, the AI story is credible and structurally advantaged over AppLovin, and there is a real dated catalyst, but the roughly 161% re-rating means the market already recognizes all of it, so there is no margin of safety in the price. On the question that matters most for comparison: Unity is considerably weaker and riskier than AppLovin on the financials today, but it has the higher growth trajectory and a genuine second engine (game creation) that AppLovin structurally lacks.

## Growth

The headline is the opposite of AppLovin: weakening in level of profitability but strengthening and accelerating in growth.

Unity reports two businesses. **Grow** is its advertising and monetization arm, powered by its AI engine, Unity Vector. **Create** is its game engine, which helps developers build and run games. This two-sided structure is the core of the story, because it means AI helps Unity twice. As AI makes games cheaper and easier to build, more games get made, which drives Create usage, and the resulting flood of new games makes discovery harder, which drives Grow. AppLovin, by contrast, only participates in the second half of that loop.

The growth is real and accelerating on the strategic (continuing) business:

- Strategic revenue grew 35% year-over-year in Q1 2026 and 38% in Q2 2026, with Q3 guided to 44 to 47%, an acceleration (Unity Q1 and Q2 FY2026 calls).
- Strategic Grow (the ad business) grew 49% in Q1 and 63% in Q2, guided to 68 to 70% in Q3 (Unity calls).
- Unity Vector, the AI ad engine, grew 15% sequentially for four straight quarters, then jumped to 23% in Q2, well above the 12 to 13% management expected. It reached a $1 billion-plus annual run rate two quarters ahead of plan (Unity Q2 FY2026 call).
- Create grew in the mid-teens for four straight quarters (15% in Q1, 14% in Q2), guided to slow to 7 to 10% in Q3 (Unity calls).

One important nuance: the *reported total* revenue looks nearly flat (about $2.0 billion trailing) because Unity is deliberately winding down low-value, "non-strategic" businesses (the ironSource ad network and the Supersonic publishing arm). That drag masks the fast-growing core. Non-strategic revenue fell to about $136 million in the first half of 2026 from about $204 million a year earlier and drops to roughly $20 million by Q3 (10-Q; Unity Q2 call). Stripped of that tail, the underlying business is inflecting, not stalling.

The AI product progress is concrete, not hand-wavy. In Q2, Unity shipped over 20 major Vector updates, and its Day 28 return-on-ad-spend product drove an 80% incremental lift on campaigns and a 37% better ROAS than the old 7-day benchmark, with over 25% of advertisers adopting it and spend tripling in a quarter (Unity Q1 and Q2 FY2026 calls). Late in Q2, Unity turned on its most distinctive asset for the first time: runtime data, the real-time behavioral signal from about 3 billion monthly players of Unity-made games, fed directly into the ad models. Management calls this its primary long-term moat, but note it went live only at the very end of the quarter, so it contributed nothing measurable to the strong Q2 number; it is forward optionality, not yet a proven driver (Unity Q2 FY2026 call).

## Profitability

This is the weakest part of the story and the clearest gap versus AppLovin, though it is improving quickly.

- **Unity has never been GAAP profitable.** Operating margin is about −30% trailing, and it has been negative every year for five years. Return on invested capital is about −17.7% (FMP; 10-Q). The first GAAP profit in company history is guided for Q3 2026, pulled forward from Q4 (Unity Q2 call).
- **The adjusted numbers look far better than the real ones.** Adjusted EBITDA was $160 million in Q2 at a 29% margin, up 800 basis points year-over-year, guided to 33% in Q3 (Unity Q2 call). But that margin is built by adding back large, partly recurring costs, chiefly stock-based compensation and repeated restructuring charges.
- **Stock-based comp is the key quality gap.** It runs about 17% of revenue trailing, roughly four times AppLovin's 4%. Charged properly, it roughly halves Unity's free cash flow: the headline free cash flow margin of about 27% becomes true owner earnings (free cash flow minus stock comp) of only about 10% of revenue, versus AppLovin's roughly 62% (10-Q Note 8; management confirmed; FMP). To management's credit, stock comp is falling: down 25% year-over-year and 14% of revenue in Q2, its lowest ever (Unity Q2 call).
- **The cash generation is real and rising, even if flattered.** Free cash flow was $202 million in Q2, up 59% year-over-year, and Unity crossed into a net cash position (cash about $2.36 billion versus debt about $2.24 billion) for the first time (Unity Q2 call; 10-Q).
- **A mechanical tailwind is coming.** Acquisition-related amortization drops from $117 million in Q1 2026 to under $25 million for all of 2027, which lifts reported earnings toward cash flow regardless of operations (Unity Q1 call).

One honest note in Unity's favor: the underlying gross margins are healthy software-like levels, about 82 to 83% on an adjusted basis. The reason there is no operating profit is spending, not weak unit economics: research and development alone runs about half of revenue as Unity invests in AI (10-Q; Unity Q2 call).

## Risk

The risks here are different from AppLovin's. AppLovin's risk is whether elite growth is durable; Unity's risk is whether a weaker, more expensive, still-unprofitable business justifies a stock that has already run.

- **It is priced for a clean inflection with no earnings floor.** Near a 52-week high, up about 161% off the lows, at roughly 85 times trailing owner earnings and no GAAP price-to-earnings at all (market data; FMP). If the turnaround stumbles, there is little valuation support beneath it, and the analyst low target is about $33, roughly 23% down (analyst consensus).
- **The moat it markets most is unquantified optionality, not a proven driver.** Management repeatedly declined to size the runtime-data contribution, saying there is "no analytical value" in singling it out (Unity Q2 call Q&A). It only went live at the very end of Q2, so it added nothing measurable to results, and it appears in the SEC filings exactly once, inside a data-privacy risk factor rather than a strengths section (10-K FY2025). The competitive claim is credible, but in the numbers it is a zero contributor today. One point in its favor: asked directly, AppLovin's CEO did not claim equivalent data or rebut that the signal is unique, he reframed to "the sector isn't zero-sum" (AppLovin Q2 2026 call).
- **The balance sheet is thinner than it looks.** Goodwill is about 47% of total assets, leaving tangible book value near zero, and Unity carries about $1.5 billion of convertible notes due across 2026 and 2027. It intends to pay the November 2026 note in cash, which is a real draw on that cash pile (10-Q; Unity Q2 call).
- **Restructuring has recurred for three straight years.** Separation and impairment charges hit in 2024, 2025, and 2026 (about $214 million, $33 million, and roughly $294 million), so the clean adjusted numbers lean on excluding costs that keep coming back (10-Q; 10-K FY2025).
- **AppLovin is the scale leader in the shared ad niche.** Unity Vector is about a $1 billion business against AppLovin's roughly $7.7 billion in ads, and AppLovin is far more profitable (about 84% adjusted EBITDA margin versus Unity's 29%, and 4% stock comp versus 14%). Unity is the faster-growing challenger, but off a base about one-seventh the size, and management even conceded AppLovin's cloud costs are structurally lower (Unity Q1 call Q&A).
- **It shares AppLovin's platform dependence.** Both live under Apple and Google app-store rules that govern distribution, fees, and ad data. The runtime moat is doubly exposed here: it is behavioral, regulated personal data gated by a roughly 90% developer opt-in, so tighter privacy rules or an ATT-style change would hit the asset and the risk at the same time (10-K FY2025).
- **The Create pricing model is changing.** Unity is shifting toward consumption and agent-based pricing as AI reshapes how the engine is used, an evolution that could be bumpy and could pressure seat revenue (Unity Q1 call).
- **No net revenue retention is disclosed.** The standard software durability metric is missing, so Create's stickiness cannot be independently verified.

## Narrative

Unity spent two years digging out of a hole, and it is now arguably the most convincing AI turnaround in its space. Two years ago its engine business was in decline and its ad business was broken. Today the CEO, Matt Bromberg, opened the Q2 call by saying Unity had just delivered "arguably the best quarter in Unity's history as a public company" and that the company was "done looking backwards" (Unity Q2 FY2026 call). The stock has risen about 161% off its 2024 lows to sit near a 52-week high, and the sell-side has been steadily raising targets on Vector's momentum.

The heart of the story is a flywheel that the CEO explains persuasively, and that is genuinely differentiated from AppLovin. Unity makes the tools to build games (Create) and the tools to get them discovered and monetized (Grow, powered by Vector). AI improves both sides at once. As Bromberg puts it, "creation is the new consumption," and AI will let tens of millions of new people build interactive entertainment, expanding the whole market Unity sells into. More games mean more engine usage, and more games also make discovery harder, which drives more advertising. On top of that sits Unity's most distinctive asset: runtime data from about 3 billion monthly players of Unity-made games, a real-time, sequential behavioral signal that Bromberg argues AppLovin structurally cannot match because "if you're Meta, you own your apps; if you're us, you have access to the 3 billion people playing" (Unity Q2 FY2026 call). Where AppLovin's CEO brushed aside the runtime question on his own call, notably without claiming equivalent data or rebutting that the signal is unique, Unity's leaned into it as the core of the thesis. The honest caveat is that this moat is still unquantified optionality: management refuses to size it, it went live only at the end of Q2, and it appears in the filings just once, as a data-privacy risk. It is a reason the ceiling could be higher, not a number you can bank today.

Crucially, the early data supports the story rather than contradicting it. Vector's growth beat management's own expectation and looks organic, not borrowed from the businesses being shut down. Developer sign-ups grew at the fastest rate since 2020, new Made-with-Unity game releases rose, and 90% of developers already report using AI in their workflows (Unity Q1 and Q2 FY2026 calls). Unity also landed marquee validation in the quarter, signing a multi-year engine partnership with Netflix and co-investing in the measurement company AppsFlyer alongside Meta and Google. This is a narrative with momentum and credible backers.

The honest counterweight is that the market already knows all of this. Unity is still not GAAP profitable, its stock comp is about four times AppLovin's as a share of revenue, its balance sheet leans on goodwill with a wall of convertible notes to clear, and the runtime moat it sells hardest is the one it refuses to put a number on. The stock is priced for the clean inflection to continue, which leaves little room for error.

The whole debate resolves at one event. Around early November, the Q3 report will either deliver Unity's first-ever GAAP profit with strategic revenue up 44 to 47%, proving the flywheel and the margin turn are real and durable, or it will slip and expose a premium-priced, still-lossmaking business to a fast de-rating. Unlike AppLovin, where the question is whether elite growth is decelerating, Unity's question is whether a genuinely exciting, AI-advantaged, but financially weaker business can finish crossing into sustained profitability on the schedule it just promised.
