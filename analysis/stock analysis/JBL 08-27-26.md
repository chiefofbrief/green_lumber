## Synthesis

**Growth.** TTM sales are $33.59B, up 17.8% YoY, a sharp annual turnaround from outright declines two years ago (TTM growth moved from -13.9% to -6.1% to +17.8% across the last three years). Growth is overwhelmingly driven by Intelligent Infrastructure and the AI data-center buildout — AI-related revenue is guided to ~$13.6B in FY2026 (+50% YoY) with a third hyperscaler win now converting into future revenue — and is almost entirely organic, not acquisition-driven. Continuation looks reasonably well-supported: management is investing real capital in new capacity (Memphis, North Carolina, India), the AI S-curve looks early-to-mid stage rather than mature, and guidance implies Q4 FY26 growth reaccelerating to ~16% YoY. The one nuance worth holding onto is that the most recently reported quarter (Q3 FY26) actually decelerated sharply versus Q2 (23.1% → 11.8% YoY growth) even as full-year guidance was raised — a reminder that the annual growth story and the most recent quarterly trend are not telling identical stories right now.

**Profitability.** Gross margin is structurally thin (9.2% TTM, below peer Celestica's 11.6%) — normal for an EMS/manufacturing-services model with pass-through materials costs and negligible R&D intensity (0.1% of sales) — but returns on capital are strong (ROIC 28.3% TTM) and FCF conversion has improved steadily and now runs ahead of peer Celestica's (4.5% vs. 3.3% of sales), aided by a deliberately asset-light capex model (net capex guided at just 1.0–2.0% of revenue). Leverage is low and well within an investment-grade discipline (debt-to-core-EBITDA of 1.3x), and accounting quality looks clean to conservative — OCF has run well ahead of net income in every one of the last five years, meaning GAAP earnings likely understate cash generation rather than the reverse. The main profitability watch-items are a genuine, unresolved question about the FY2027 margin trajectory (a 6.4% guided Q4 FY26 exit versus only a "6% plus" FY2027 target) and a rising goodwill balance from an active bolt-on M&A program.

**Risk.** The most idiosyncratic risk is broad-based, one-directional insider selling — the CEO and multiple senior officers sold roughly $14.2M net over six months with essentially no offsetting buying, and this analysis could not confirm whether the sales were pre-arranged under Rule 10b5-1 plans. Customer concentration is present but only partially verifiable from the available data (top-5 customers at 36% of revenue, stable; a more granular named-customer table referenced in the 10-K was not captured in the extracted text). Debt, covenant, and liquidity risk are low. Automotive/EV demand (particularly China) and component supply chain (HBM, high-density-interconnect PCBs) are the clearest external risks management itself has flagged as sources of near-term volatility.

**Affordability.** After a roughly two-year, +160.8%-off-trough re-rating (with a further +222% peak in June 2026 before a ~19% pullback), the stock trades at an EV/Sales of 1.05x — notably cheaper than peer Celestica's 2.37x despite comparable-or-better FCF conversion — alongside a GAAP P/E of 38.46x and adjusted P/E of 25.98x. The sell side is decisively bullish (0% Sell ratings across 23 analysts, average price targets well above the current price), while a smaller but vocal set of skeptics (GuruFocus's GF Value framework, one Seeking Alpha Hold call) argues the stock has become significantly overvalued on trailing-multiple grounds. Management's own capital-allocation choices — continuing large buybacks and stating directly that it views the shares as undervalued — line up more closely with the bull case than the bear case, and this analysis's own read sits closer to that constructive middle ground than to either extreme.

## How does the company make money?

Jabil is a global electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company: customers hand it a product specification and Jabil designs (in some cases), builds, tests, and manages the supply chain for the physical product, then delivers it back to the customer under a manufacturing services contract. Revenue is earned almost entirely from these "manufacturing services" — producing tangible components and finished products built to customer specifications — plus a smaller amount from standalone electronic design services (ASIC design, firmware, rapid prototyping, mechanical/CAD, validation and testing) (JBL_10k.txt).

The company organizes itself into three reportable segments. **Regulated Industries** serves automotive & transportation, healthcare & packaging, and renewables & energy infrastructure customers. **Intelligent Infrastructure** serves the "modern digital ecosystem," including AI infrastructure, capital equipment, cloud & data center infrastructure, and networking & communications. **Connected Living & Digital Commerce (CL&DC)** covers connected devices plus digitalization/automation — warehouse automation and robotics (JBL_10k.txt).

For FY2025, the segment mix was close to even between the two largest: Intelligent Infrastructure $12.32B (41.3% of revenue), Regulated Industries $11.88B (39.9%), and CL&DC $5.61B (18.8%) (JBL_numbers.md). That mix has since shifted decisively toward Intelligent Infrastructure — one data point puts II at roughly 48% of revenue by the June 2026 quarter (JBL_data_summary.md), and the Q3 FY26 print confirms it: Intelligent Infrastructure revenue of $4.2B outweighed Regulated Industries' $3.2B and CL&DC's $1.4B (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). Intelligent Infrastructure is now the clearly dominant and fastest-growing segment.

Operationally, most Jabil business units serve a single customer, with dedicated production lines, staff, and engineers assigned to that relationship — a "customer-centric business unit" model the company says promotes accountability and lets it scale incrementally without disrupting other units (JBL_10k.txt). This differs from a pure job-shop assembler: Jabil's value-add design services and its willingness to co-invest in customer-specific capacity (e.g., customers sometimes co-invest in PP&E, or provide inventory deposits) let it capture more of the value chain than commodity contract assembly (JBL_10k.txt).

## What are its competitive advantages?

Jabil does not present itself as a pure low-cost producer. Its 10-K lists cost as just one of several competitive factors alongside "accelerated production time-to-market; higher efficiencies; global locations; rapid scaling of production; advanced technologies; quality; and improved pricing of components," and claims broad competitiveness across all of them rather than price leadership specifically (JBL_10k.txt) — a generic, self-reported claim rather than an independently verified one.

**IP is modest, by the company's own admission.** Jabil states it has "a relatively modest number of patents" and relies primarily on unpatented know-how, trade secrets, and non-disclosure agreements rather than a patent moat (JBL_10k.txt). This is corroborated quantitatively: R&D is just 0.1% of sales on a TTM basis ($0.03B against $33.59B of revenue) (JBL_numbers.md) — a tenth of peer Celestica's 1.0% R&D/Sales (JBL_numbers.md peer table). Jabil is structurally not an R&D-intensity or patent-driven business.

Jabil also has no meaningful consumer brand or distribution advantage — it is a B2B manufacturer whose relationships are built on multi-year contracts and dedicated business units, not marketing. Its global manufacturing footprint (135,000 employees across facilities including the US, China, Malaysia, Mexico, and India) (profile.md) functions as a substitute for a distribution advantage: it lets Jabil manufacture near end markets and offer customers geographic diversification, which matters given 75.8% (Q3) and 73.8% (nine months) of net revenue came from international operations (JBL_10q.txt), and management explicitly cites Jabil's U.S.-domicile manufacturing as something customers actively want amid tariff and onshoring pressure (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md).

Given the low R&D spend, Jabil's real differentiator, per management's own framing, is **system-level integration capability rather than component IP or effectiveness at traditional R&D**: it describes a "holistic strategy" that is "not product-based" but instead combines compute, networking, power distribution, and advanced (including liquid) cooling into one integrated system for a customer, entering a relationship through a single capability and then expanding into the full stack — the stated playbook behind both its second and third hyperscaler wins (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md; JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). Combined with an asset-light delivery model in Intelligent Infrastructure (net capex guided to just 1.5–2.0% of revenue) (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md) and long-standing, multi-year customer relationships that raise switching costs, this is a credible but largely execution-and-scale-based moat rather than a hard-to-replicate technological one — competitors such as Flex, Celestica, and Sanmina pursue similar programs, and the "holistic/system-level" pitch is self-described by management rather than independently verified [INFERRED: JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md/2026Q3.md, management's own framing of its win rationale].

## How and why are sales growing?

TTM sales are $33.59B, up 17.8% YoY (JBL_numbers.md). On an **annual** basis this represents a sharp turnaround, not steady growth: TTM revenue growth has gone from -13.9% (TTM to Q3 FY24) to -6.1% (TTM to Q3 FY25) to +17.8% (TTM to Q3 FY26) — each step an acceleration (-24.6pp → +7.7pp → +23.9pp) (JBL_numbers.md). Two years ago the business was still shrinking; today it is growing at a high-teens rate.

On a **quarterly** basis the picture is more mixed and worth reading carefully rather than assuming a straight line up. YoY growth by quarter was: Q3 FY25 ~15.7% (implied) → Q4 FY25 +18.5% (accel +2.8pp) → Q1 FY26 +18.7% (accel +0.2pp) → Q2 FY26 +23.1% (accel +4.4pp) → Q3 FY26 +11.8% (accel **-11.3pp**) (JBL_numbers.md). So growth accelerated for three straight quarters, then decelerated sharply in the most recent reported quarter — even though Q3 FY26 revenue still beat the company's own guidance by $250M (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). Management's Q4 FY26 guide of $9.2–10.0B implies growth reaccelerating to roughly 16% YoY at the midpoint (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md), but the most recent single-quarter data point is a deceleration, not a straight-line acceleration.

Growth is overwhelmingly driven by **Intelligent Infrastructure**, and within it, by the AI data-center buildout: II revenue was up 52% YoY in Q2 FY26 and 21% YoY in Q3 FY26 (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md; JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md), and full-year AI-related revenue is now guided to ~$13.6B for FY2026, up from $9B in FY2025 — roughly 50% growth — driven by cloud/data-center infrastructure, networking & communications (helped by a strong India ramp), and capital equipment (automated test equipment demand, and a nascent recovery in wafer-fab equipment) (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). The growth is broad-based within the segment rather than a single product line, and is reinforced by two hyperscaler wins (the third closed in Q3 FY26) plus the Hanley acquisition, which adds higher-margin data-center power/energy capabilities (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). Regulated Industries is a secondary tailwind: Automotive & Transportation and Renewables are both recovering off multi-year lows, aided by a shift to powertrain-agnostic platforms and stronger export demand from China (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). CL&DC is mixed — Connected Living is roughly flat/declining on planned program attrition, while Digital Commerce (warehouse automation, robotics, retail automation) continues to grow at what management calls a "double-digit" rate and is one of the company's higher-margin end markets (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md).

Growth is predominantly **organic**. Jabil does not separately break out acquisition-driven revenue, stating that the added cost structure from acquisitions "has historically been relatively insignificant" relative to overall scale (JBL_10k.txt) — and this is corroborated for the largest recent deal: Hanley Energy Group ($752M, closed January 2026) accounted for only 3.8% of total assets and less than 1.0% of net revenue over the nine months ended May 31, 2026 (JBL_10q.txt). The other recent, smaller acquisitions (Rebound Technologies, Pii, Mikros Technologies) are similarly capability additions rather than material revenue contributors. The AI-infrastructure growth story is a demand story, not an M&A roll-up.

## Is there a reasonable expectation of accelerated or continued future sales growth?

On balance, yes — the evidence supports continued (though not guaranteed to accelerate) growth, with real, disclosed forward drivers, tempered by supply-chain and margin-timing caveats management itself raises.

**Sector positioning.** Jabil sits inside AI infrastructure hardware, which this pipeline's own sector thesis places in a "mid-late acceleration" stage — still growing, but a framework that also expects hardware to eventually commoditize and favor low-cost producers over time (Sectors.md). That long-run dynamic arguably favors a high-volume, lower-R&D-intensity manufacturer like Jabil more than it favors a design-IP-heavy peer, though it also means today's above-average margins in Intelligent Infrastructure are not guaranteed to be durable as the space matures.

**S-curve stage.** Within that broader sector, Jabil's own AI programs look earlier-stage than the "mid-late" label alone suggests: AI-related revenue is still growing ~50% YoY off a fast-rising base ($9B FY25 → $13.6B FY26 guided, with UBS projecting $20.3B in FY27) (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md; JBL_data_summary.md), and the newest capacity (North Carolina, Memphis expansion, India) is only just coming online — management expects substantial new capacity to be operational by early calendar 2027 (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md).

**Market share.** No independent market-share figure is disclosed. The company's own evidence is the customer-win cadence: a second hyperscaler ramping "very positively," and a third hyperscaler win closed in Q3 FY26, expected to contribute a "couple hundred million" in FY2027 before rapidly scaling toward $1B+ by FY2028 (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md) — directionally supportive of share gains but not independently verified [INFERRED: JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md, management's own account].

**Moat durability.** As discussed above, the moat is execution/system-integration and scale, reinforced by long customer relationships and an asset-light capex model, rather than patented technology — durable enough to explain repeat hyperscaler wins, but not structurally unique in an industry with several credible EMS competitors chasing the same programs.

**Investment commitment.** Management is putting real capital and management attention behind the thesis, not just riding favorable end-market exposure: Memphis expansion (+1.5M sq ft), a new North Carolina facility (on track for a fully-ramped state by around January 2027, with adjacent land secured for faster future expansion), India capacity growth (footprint up from 500,000 to 1.2M sq ft in a year, plus a new Pune factory), and a $119M/2,200-job Marshall County, Mississippi investment specifically to serve data-center infrastructure customers (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md; JBL_data_summary.md). Net capex is guided up modestly to 1.5–2.0% of revenue for FY2027 (from 1.0–1.5% in FY2026) even while staying asset-light (JBL_10q.txt).

**AI/technology leverage.** Beyond selling into AI infrastructure, Jabil is also an early, small-scale participant in physical AI/robotics (warehouse automation, humanoids) within Digital Commerce, and describes using AI internally for manufacturing quality/corrective-action processes across its global site network (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md). Both are described as longer-term, still-early opportunities rather than current material drivers.

**Management commitment.** Leadership is explicit and consistent about prioritizing the AI-infrastructure opportunity — repeatedly raising full-year AI revenue guidance (from $13.1B to $13.6B this year alone), reiterating confidence in >6% core operating margin for FY2027, and pursuing a longer-dated, still-unstructured India AI-manufacturing alliance with Adani Enterprises (explicitly framed as an FY2028 event, with "no definitive framework" yet on capital structure) (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md).

**Challenges.** Several are disclosed directly by management rather than inferred: (1) component supply — High Bandwidth Memory and high-density interconnect PCBs are described as tightening, with extending lead times, though hyperscaler customers tend to secure their own allocations (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md); (2) Automotive/EV demand remains volatile, with China specifically called out as soft even as other regions recover, and management says it is staying deliberately "cautious" and "conservative" on both Automotive and Renewables despite recent upside (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md; JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md); (3) margin-timing risk on new capacity — Q4 FY26 exits at a guided 6.4% core margin, but FY2027 guidance is only "6% plus" for now, and at least one analyst pressed management directly on how to reconcile the two, given startup costs from newly ramping capacity (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md); and (4) CL&DC's Connected Living sub-segment remains structurally soft (planned program attrition), a drag that must be offset by Digital Commerce and the two growth segments.

**Stop.** Ask the user's permission before Pass 2.

## Is it highly dependent on a small number of customers?

Jabil discloses meaningful but not extreme concentration, and it appears less concentrated than at least one direct EMS peer on the metrics available. Its five largest customers accounted for approximately 36% of net revenue in both FY2025 and the nine months ended May 31, 2026 — a stable figure — while the number of customers needed to reach 90% of revenue narrowed slightly, from 87 (FY2025) to 78 (nine months FY2026) (JBL_10k.txt; JBL_10q.txt). Both filings reference a table breaking out named-customer concentration in more detail, but that table's actual figures were not captured in the extracted text available for this analysis — a real gap: this analysis cannot confirm whether any single customer individually exceeds a reporting threshold (e.g., 10% of revenue), only that the top-5 aggregate is 36% [gap: named single-customer concentration table not available in extracted filing text]. By contrast, peer Celestica separately discloses that its single largest customer alone is roughly a third of its total revenue — if Jabil had a comparably concentrated single customer, the risk-factor language would likely be more specific than the aggregate "five largest customers" framing it uses, so the more diversified read is the more likely one, but it is not confirmed.

Jabil's own risk-factor language is explicit that this concentration is structural, not incidental: "we currently depend, and expect to continue to depend for the foreseeable future, upon a relatively small number of customers for a significant percentage of our net revenue," and notes that in some cases a single manufacturing program can represent a significant share of the revenue from that customer, compounding program-level risk on top of customer-level risk (JBL_10k.txt). Customers also do not generally commit to production schedules beyond one quarter, which the company flags as a factor that makes capacity planning and utilization difficult even where the customer relationship itself is not at risk (JBL_10k.txt).

Diversification comes primarily from Jabil's three-segment, multi-industry structure (automotive, healthcare/packaging, renewables, cloud/data-center infrastructure, networking, capital equipment, connected devices, digital commerce) rather than from a broad, undifferentiated customer base — management explicitly frames this diversified end-market mix as a deliberate hedge against a single sector's downturn, a strategy point echoed across both the FY26 Q2 and Q3 calls (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md; JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). One large, named relationship stands out structurally: Jabil issued a common-stock warrant to Amazon.com NV Investment Holdings LLC, accounted for as an equity instrument and amortized as a reduction to revenue over the warrant term (JBL_10k.txt) — an equity-linked customer arrangement that signals a strategically important, large relationship, consistent with Amazon being repeatedly named in analyst commentary as Jabil's most significant AI/hyperscaler collaborator (JBL_data_summary.md). A Jabil board member (James Siminoff) also returned to an Amazon executive role during FY2025, creating a disclosed but immaterial related-party relationship (JBL_10k.txt).

## Does it have a worthwhile gross margin? If not, is there a good reason why the margin is low?

Gross margin is thin in absolute terms — TTM gross margin is 9.2%, versus a 5-year trough of 7.9% (FY2022) and a 5-year average of 8.5% (JBL_numbers.md) — but this is normal for the EMS/contract-manufacturing model, where cost of revenue is dominated by pass-through component and materials costs. Against the one peer in the data, Jabil's 9.2% TTM gross margin is meaningfully below Celestica's 11.6% (JBL_numbers.md peer table), reflecting Celestica's heavier mix of higher-value, co-designed (HPS) work versus Jabil's broader, more traditional manufacturing-services mix.

The current margin sits above the 5-year average but below the FY2024 peak of 9.3% (annual sequence: 8.1% → 7.9% → 8.3% → 9.3% → 8.9% → 9.2% TTM) (JBL_numbers.md). The FY2025 dip versus FY2024 was explicitly attributed by the company to "product mix in our Connected Living and Digital Commerce and Intelligent Infrastructure segments" (JBL_10k.txt) — i.e., a mix headwind, not a cost-control failure — while the more recent nine-month FY2026 improvement is attributed to favorable "product mix in our Intelligent Infrastructure segment" (JBL_10q.txt), consistent with Intelligent Infrastructure adding higher-margin capabilities (power distribution and services via Hanley, liquid cooling via Mikros, silicon photonics) as it scales. Quarterly gross margin has also recovered to 9.5% in Q3 FY26 after dipping to 8.9–9.0% in Q1–Q2 FY26 (JBL_numbers.md).

The margin is not being depressed by reinvestment into R&D or sales — R&D is negligible (0.1% of sales, per Pass 1) — so the thin gross margin is simply structural to the manufacturing-services business model rather than a temporary trade-off for growth spending. Management does not guide to gross margin specifically; its stated focus and public guidance metric is **core operating margin**, currently ~5.8% for FY2026 and targeted at "6% and beyond" for FY2027, with management repeatedly expressing high confidence in that trajectory, driven by improving mix (power/liquid cooling/silicon photonics), rising capacity utilization (75% a year ago to 80% today), and operating leverage on a larger revenue base (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md; JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). Read alongside a TTM ROIC of 28.3% — well above the segment's cost of capital and roughly in line with the 5-year average of 30.2% (JBL_numbers.md) — the thin gross margin looks less like a quality problem and more like a structural feature of a business that is nonetheless generating strong returns on the capital it deploys.

## Is it generating FCF? If not, is there a good reason why?

Yes, and FCF generation has been improving steadily and now compares favorably to the one peer in the data. TTM FCF/Sales is 4.5%, versus a 5-year trough of 0.8% (FY2022) and a 5-year average of 2.2% (JBL_numbers.md) — a clean, multi-year upward trend (0.9% → 0.8% → 2.0% → 3.2% → 3.9% → 4.5% TTM). Against peer Celestica's 3.3% TTM FCF/Sales, Jabil currently converts a larger share of sales to free cash flow despite its lower gross margin — a function of Jabil running a genuinely lighter capex model (CapEx/D&A has fallen to 50.6% TTM from a 5-year average of 115.1%, meaning capex now runs at roughly half of depreciation) (JBL_numbers.md) versus Celestica, which is currently running capex at roughly 6% of sales to build out AI-infrastructure capacity.

FCF is not being held back by growth reinvestment; if anything, the opposite dynamic is at work — Jabil deliberately runs an asset-light model, with net capex guided at just 1.0–1.5% of revenue for FY2026, stepping up only modestly to 1.5–2.0% for FY2027 to fund the new Memphis, North Carolina, and India capacity discussed in Pass 1 (JBL_10q.txt). Management is explicit that this asset-light posture in Intelligent Infrastructure specifically is a deliberate return-on-capital choice — avoiding "the product ownership and IP risk that can come with more OEM-like models" — rather than underinvestment (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md).

Quarter-to-quarter FCF is volatile on working-capital timing: adjusted FCF was $420M (Q4 FY25), $230M (Q1 FY26), $510M (Q2 FY26), and $350M (Q3 FY26) (JBL_numbers.md), and inventory days ran to 84 gross (68 net of customer deposits) in Q3 FY26, above the company's normal 55–60 day target range, which management attributes to the timing of Intelligent Infrastructure customer shipments and expects to normalize in Q4 (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). This working-capital swing shows up starkly in the annual data too — reported working capital moved from +$1.01B (FY2024) to essentially $0.01B (FY2025) to -$0.35B (TTM), with a 5-year CV of 1.22 (JBL_numbers.md) — a genuinely volatile line that is worth flagging, though it looks more like active working-capital management (customer deposits, receivables factoring, extended payables — see the accounting-quality question below) than a going-concern signal, since it has coincided with rising, not falling, FCF.

Management's guidance for future cash flow is confident and has been raised intra-year: full-year FY2026 adjusted FCF guidance moved from ">$1.3 billion" to ">$1.4 billion" as of the Q3 FY26 call (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md), and capital allocation is disciplined — roughly 80% of FCF into share buybacks, about 20% into smaller "capability" M&A, with management noting it has leverage capacity to pursue larger M&A "if the right type of deal" appeared, while remaining committed to an investment-grade credit profile (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md).

## Are liabilities and expenses reasonable and manageable?

Overall, yes — leverage is low and well within the company's own investment-grade discipline, though one leverage metric (interest coverage) has declined and deserves context. Debt/Sales sits at 11.6% TTM, close to the 5-year average of 10.7%, and Debt/OCF is a low 2.10x, close to its 5-year average of 2.04x (JBL_numbers.md). Management itself cites debt-to-core-EBITDA of just 1.3x as of Q3 FY26 (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md) — a genuinely low leverage level. The company ended Q3 FY26 with $1.4B in cash and $4.4B of available unused borrowing capacity across its revolving credit facilities and receivables financing facility (JBL_10q.txt), and states it has remained in compliance with all debt covenants across the periods disclosed (JBL_10k.txt; JBL_10q.txt) — this, combined with steadily rising FCF (above), indicates ample cushion to service debt even in a downturn.

Interest coverage did decline meaningfully — from an 11.1x peak in FY2024 to 4.7x in FY2025 and 4.6x TTM (5-year average 8.0x) (JBL_numbers.md) — but this is largely a comparison-base effect rather than a fresh deterioration: FY2024's abnormally high coverage was inflated by the $942M pretax gain on the Mobility Business divestiture, which lifted that year's operating income (operating margin peaked at 7.0% in FY2024 before falling back to 4.0% in FY2025) (JBL_numbers.md; JBL_10k.txt). The more recent trend is improving on a quarterly basis (4.0x → 4.0x → 4.6x → 5.8x, Q4 FY25 through Q3 FY26) (JBL_numbers.md), even as total debt and interest expense both rose from financing the Hanley acquisition and refinancing maturing notes ($500M of 4.200% and $500M of 4.750% Senior Notes issued January 2026, partly to repay maturing 1.700% notes) (JBL_10q.txt) — a normal, disclosed cost of funding growth and M&A rather than a sign of strain.

Stock-based compensation is small and stable (0.3% of sales TTM, matching the 5-year average) (JBL_numbers.md) and not a meaningful drag on reported earnings. Capex is, if anything, shrinking as a share of the business (D&A/Sales down to 2.1% TTM from 3.0% in FY2021) (JBL_numbers.md), consistent with the deliberately asset-light posture discussed above, though it is guided to rise modestly in FY2027 to fund new capacity. SG&A/Sales is stable at 3.9% TTM versus a 3.8% five-year average (JBL_numbers.md) — recent dollar increases are tied to salary costs from the Hanley, Rebound Technologies, and Pii acquisitions (JBL_10q.txt), but as a share of revenue, expense discipline looks intact; the company does not separately break out sales & marketing spend from SG&A in the available data, so it cannot be isolated as a distinct line item.

Off-balance-sheet items are modest relative to scale: $176M of lease payments for signed-but-not-commenced leases, $144M of maximum exposure to a variable-interest-entity lease, and $280M of residual value guarantees the company does not consider probable to come due (JBL_10k.txt). No covenant violations, pension shortfalls, or JV guarantee issues surfaced in the available filings.

## Do accounting choices appear to be inflating or depressing reported earnings?

The master check here points the opposite direction from a typical earnings-quality concern: OCF/Net Income is TTM 2.15x, and has stayed above 1.2x in every one of the last five fiscal years (2.06x → 1.66x → 2.12x → 1.24x → 2.50x → 2.15x TTM) (JBL_numbers.md). Operating cash flow running well ahead of net income — rather than trailing it — is a conservative-accounting signal, not a red flag; it mainly reflects that GAAP net income is loaded with real but non-cash or non-recurring charges (intangible amortization, stock-based compensation, restructuring/severance) that don't reduce cash generation. This is also why the gap between GAAP and the company's "core" (non-GAAP) earnings figures is wide and worth understanding rather than simply accepting: Jabil's core operating income excludes amortization of intangibles, SBC, restructuring/severance, distressed-customer charges, divestiture gains/losses, goodwill impairment, debt-extinguishment losses, and several other items (JBL_10k.txt) — for Q3 FY26 alone, GAAP operating income ($445M) sat noticeably below core operating income ($504M) (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md), and the FY2026 core EPS guide of ~$12.70 is materially higher than what GAAP EPS is likely to be once the excluded items are included.

Running through the framework's specific checks: **revenue quality** looks clean — no signs of channel stuffing or premature recognition; rising receivables, inventory, and contract-asset balances in the 10-Q are explicitly and consistently attributed to "timing of customer shipments in the Intelligent Infrastructure segment," a routine, disclosed operational driver rather than an unexplained anomaly (JBL_10q.txt). **Reserves/timing** shows one item worth flagging: "non-recurring" restructuring charges have in fact recurred in back-to-back plans — the 2024 Restructuring Plan (~$300M pretax) and the 2025 Restructuring Plan (~$200M pretax), the latter still substantially completing through FY2026 (JBL_10k.txt; JBL_10q.txt) — a pattern the framework flags as "an operating cost in disguise" when it repeats, though at under 1% of revenue per year the amounts are modest relative to Jabil's scale. **Non-GAAP/SBC**: SBC is real and expensed, and is included as a normal excluded item in core earnings — standard EMS-industry practice, not aggressive. **Tax**: FY2025 and FY2024 effective tax rates were both affected by one-off items (a benefit from the lapse of a statute of limitations in FY2025; tax expense tied to the Mobility Business divestiture gain in FY2024) (JBL_10k.txt), but management's consistent use of a normalized ~21% "core tax rate" for guidance reduces (without eliminating) the risk that reported EPS growth is being flattered by tax-rate noise. **Acquisitions**: Jabil has pursued a steady cadence of small, capability-driven bolt-ons (ProcureAbility, Mikros, Pii, Rebound Technologies, Hanley), each individually described as immaterial to consolidated results, with no large write-offs or "big bath" impairments in the data — but goodwill is genuinely rising as a result: Goodwill/Sales is 3.7% TTM versus a 2.3% five-year average, and the quarterly ratio jumped to 14.0–14.8% of quarterly revenue in Q2–Q3 FY26 as the $340M of Hanley goodwill was added (JBL_numbers.md; JBL_10q.txt) — not a current impairment concern, but a real, rising balance-sheet exposure that would need re-checking if any of these acquired businesses underperform. **Cash-flow presentation**: Jabil runs sizable receivables-sale and asset-backed securitization programs — $11.4B of trade AR sold in FY2025 alone under the global ABS program, plus billions more under separate trade-AR sale programs — but the outstanding balance where Jabil retains continuing involvement has been roughly stable-to-declining ($927M → $421M, FY2025 year-end to Q3 FY26) rather than sharply escalating (JBL_10k.txt; JBL_10q.txt), so this looks like an established, disclosed working-capital tool rather than a recent lever being pulled harder to flatter OCF. **Off-balance-sheet/leverage**: covered above; modest and disclosed. **Governance flags**: none identified — the one related-party disclosure (a board member's return to an Amazon executive role) was flagged and deemed immaterial (JBL_10k.txt), and no auditor change or unusual audit-fee pattern appears in the available data.

Net read: nothing here suggests earnings are being inflated. If anything, GAAP earnings understate underlying cash generation, and the main things worth watching are the size of the GAAP-to-core earnings bridge (a legitimate but real gap the market should not treat as free) and the rising goodwill balance from an active bolt-on M&A program.

## Is management trustworthy?

CEO Michael Dastoor is a long-tenured internal promotion, not an outside hire riding the AI cycle: he joined Jabil in 2000, spent 2010–2018 as Senior VP & Corporate Controller and 2018–2024 as CFO before being named CEO in May 2024, succeeding Kenneth Wilson (JBL_management.md) — 24+ years of institutional knowledge, including through the company's much harder years (FY2023–FY2024 revenue declines discussed in Pass 1).

On disclosure and tone, management reads as reasonably open. On the Q3 FY26 call it gave specific, granular answers to pointed analyst questions — reconciling a guided Q4 exit margin of 6.4% against a "6% plus" FY2027 target, explaining exactly how much of the Intelligent Infrastructure guidance raise was hyperscaler-specific versus broad-based, and quantifying the third hyperscaler's expected near-term contribution ("couple-hundred-million range" in FY2027, scaling toward "a billion and then beyond" in FY2028) rather than deflecting to vague optimism (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). On the not-yet-finalized India/Adani opportunity, the CEO was explicit about the limits of what he could say — "we do not have a definitive framework in place yet... I can't comment on financials or structure" — rather than promoting an unstructured deal as a firm growth driver (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). Notably, the CEO also volunteered a specific caution to analysts pushing for more aggressive FY2027 framing: "Let's exercise some caution; I want to make sure that we don't get carried away with the numbers" (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md) — a concrete instance of management tempering enthusiasm rather than hyping a raise, a modest positive trust signal. Tone did not shift defensively between the Q2 and Q3 FY26 calls; if anything management grew more specific over that period (naming the third hyperscaler win, disclosing the Adani discussions, giving an early FY2027 AI-revenue framework ahead of formal September guidance), which is more consistent with genuine improving visibility than with promotional inflation, though this cannot be fully separated from optimism bias since it is management's own framing [INFERRED: JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md; JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md].

Insider trading is the clearest caution flag. Over the last six months, insiders bought just $149,815 of stock (one purchase, by a director) against $14.37M sold across 26 separate sales — a net of -$14.22M (JBL_management.md). The selling is broad-based across the leadership team, not concentrated in one person: CEO Michael Dastoor sold roughly $2.7M on April 8; EVP Steven Borges sold over $5.7M across three separate April transactions; EVP Andrew Priestley sold $1.05M and $1.20M in April; the CHRO, an EVP, two SVPs, the head of Investor Relations, and a director all sold as well, with only a single small purchase ($150K, by a director in July) offsetting any of it (JBL_management.md). Much of the selling clustered in an April 8–20 window and again in mid-July, periods that overlap with the stock's rapid climb from the $270s–$300s toward the $340s–$380s (JBL_numbers.md price table) — consistent with executives opportunistically monetizing a fast re-rating, which is common and not inherently evidence of misconduct. However, unlike the peer analysis in this pipeline, the available Jabil filings excerpts do not confirm whether these sales were executed under pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 trading plans, so this analysis cannot extend management the benefit of pre-scheduled, non-discretionary timing the way a confirmed 10b5-1 disclosure would allow [gap: 10b5-1 plan status not confirmed in the available data]. Combined with outside valuation commentary flagging the stock as significantly overvalued on some metrics (GuruFocus repeatedly, across multiple news items) (JBL_data_summary.md), broad and one-directional insider selling by the CEO and multiple senior officers, with no confirmed pre-arrangement, is a real if moderate caution worth weighing against the otherwise transparent and specific tone of management's public communication.

How accounting decisions reflect on management: favorably, on the evidence in the prior question — GAAP earnings run conservative relative to cash generation, the core-earnings exclusions are standard and disclosed rather than aggressive, and the one recurring item (back-to-back "non-recurring" restructuring plans) is modest in size and consistently explained rather than buried.

## Is the financial community's appraisal affecting the company's fundamentals?

The price has been inflated, not depressed, for a sustained period — roughly two years of large appreciation (+222% at the June 2026 peak versus the September 2024 trough, still +160.8% after the recent pullback) (JBL_numbers.md) — so the relevant question is whether that strength has fed back into company decisions, and the evidence points to a specific, informative answer: management does not appear to be treating the elevated price as a signal to raise cheap equity capital, and has said so directly.

Capital allocation is the clearest evidence. Jabil has been aggressively repurchasing stock throughout the run-up — $300M in Q2 FY26, $291M in Q3 FY26, on track to complete its existing $1B authorization in Q4, on top of a newly announced $1.5B buyback plan (July 13, 2026) and multiple accelerated share repurchase (ASR) agreements ($45M in October 2025, $200M in December 2025) (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md; JBL_data_summary.md; JBL_10q.txt). Management's own stated rationale is that "we still feel buybacks remain an excellent use of cash for Jabil as we feel our shares are undervalued" (JBL_earnings_2026Q2.md) — a direct statement that management's internal view is the opposite of GuruFocus's "significantly overvalued" framing, even well into the stock's multi-year rally. Consistent with that view, the recent acquisitions discussed throughout this analysis (Hanley $752M, Pii $309M, Rebound $134M, Mikros $63M) were all funded with cash and debt (including $1B of new Senior Notes issued in January 2026), not with newly issued equity (JBL_10q.txt) — Jabil has not used its elevated stock price as acquisition currency, in contrast to a peer like Celestica raising primary equity specifically to fund AI-infrastructure capex.

There is no evidence in the available data that the price move has altered the pace or ambition of operating decisions — the Adani/India opportunity is framed by management as capability- and market-driven rather than stock-price-driven, and the sharpest recent price pullback (June's $385 peak to August's $311) has not been accompanied by any capex pullback or guidance cut; if anything, guidance was raised at the June 17 Q3 call, before the subsequent August decline occurred. Because the price has not been depressed for any meaningful period in this window, the "is a depressed price affecting decisions or capital access" branch of this question does not apply here.

## How does the financial community's appraisal differ from our analysis? Which perspective is reflected in the price?

Our analysis in Passes 1–2 largely agrees with the bull case on growth mechanics: real, broad-based AI-infrastructure demand; a credible hyperscaler-win cadence; heavy, disclosed management investment behind the opportunity; a genuinely asset-light model generating rising FCF and strong ROIC (28.3% TTM); and clean accounting, where OCF running well ahead of net income is a conservative rather than concerning signal. On several of these points, our own read is arguably **more constructive** than GuruFocus's blanket "significantly overvalued" framing, which leans heavily on a five-year-median trailing P/E comparison that does not engage with the structural improvement in the business over that window (FCF/Sales roughly quintupling off its FY2022 trough, ROIC near its five-year average, and a real mix shift toward higher-margin AI infrastructure) — a backward-looking multiple comparison risks understating how much the earnings base itself has changed.

Where our analysis is **more cautious** than the prevailing 0%-Sell, Buy-heavy sell-side consensus: (1) we found that Q3 FY26 quarterly YoY revenue growth actually decelerated sharply (23.1% → 11.8%), a nuance that sits awkwardly next to a narrative built mainly on annual AI-revenue guidance raises; (2) customer-concentration data has a real gap — the named-customer breakdown table referenced in the 10-K was not available in the extracted text, so unlike the sell side's general comfort with Jabil's diversification, this analysis cannot independently confirm it; (3) the broad, multi-executive insider selling documented in Pass 2, with no confirmed 10b5-1 pre-arrangement, is the same fact GuruFocus cites as a caution — but it sits almost entirely outside the sell-side commentary reviewed here, which does not engage with it at all; (4) at least one analyst on the Q3 FY26 call pressed management directly on the unresolved tension between a 6.4% guided Q4 exit margin and only a "6% plus" (not more specific) FY2027 target, a live ambiguity the sell side is already probing rather than one this analysis is raising in isolation; and (5) the rising goodwill balance from Jabil's serial bolt-on M&A program (Goodwill/Sales up to 3.7% TTM from a 2.3% five-year average) does not appear anywhere in the available news or analyst commentary — a balance-sheet-quality point specific to this analysis.

On balance, the current price looks like it reflects a position **between** the two extremes rather than either one cleanly. It has not converged to the sell side's own average price targets ($400–480, implying 30–50%+ upside from $311.51) — the market has not fully adopted even the sell side's own bullishness. Nor has it moved anywhere close to GuruFocus's ~$190 GF Value estimate — the market is clearly not weighting that framework heavily either. An EV/Sales multiple of 1.05x, meaningfully cheaper than peer Celestica's 2.37x despite comparable-or-better FCF conversion (Pass 2), suggests the market may not yet be fully crediting Jabil's AI-infrastructure growth on the same terms it credits a peer more purely levered to that theme — closer to this analysis's own moderately constructive read than to either the sell side's most bullish targets or GuruFocus's bearish valuation framework. The roughly 19% pullback from the June 2026 peak into August also suggests the market has recently become somewhat more cautious — digesting the margin-reconciliation ambiguity, the insider-selling pattern, and broader AI-capex-cycle nerves — without reversing the underlying two-year re-rating.

## What catalyst(s) may force the financial community's appraisal to converge with ours?

The single most concrete, dated catalyst is management's own committed disclosure: **full FY2027 guidance at Jabil's Annual Virtual Investor Briefing in September 2026** (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). This is not a routine event — management has explicitly deferred the specifics behind its "similar percentage AI growth rate" framing, its "6% plus" FY2027 operating-margin target, and its FY2027 capex plan to that briefing, so it is the natural point at which the currently unresolved margin-reconciliation question (6.4% Q4 exit versus an unspecified "6% plus" FY2027 target, which at least one sell-side analyst already pressed management on) either gets a firm, numbers-based answer or remains a live ambiguity the market has to keep pricing around.

Beyond that, several nearer-term, falsifiable operating milestones function as real tests of the bull case rather than guidance language: the **North Carolina facility reaching its targeted "fully ramped" state around January 2027**; the **third hyperscaler's revenue materializing at the guided "couple-hundred-million" range in FY2027** before scaling toward "$1B and beyond" in FY2028; and continued **disclosed dollar contributions from Hanley and the storage/silicon-photonics ramp**, which management currently describes only qualitatively as "positive contributors" (JBL_earnings_2026Q3.md). Each of these would let the market verify the AI-infrastructure and margin-mix story with hard numbers rather than management's forward framing.

On the risk side, two catalysts could instead pull the appraisal toward the more skeptical read this analysis has flagged: (1) any resolution — in either direction — of the **High Bandwidth Memory and high-density-interconnect PCB lead-time pressures** management has already flagged as tightening, since a genuine worsening would test whether the guided growth already has adequate conservatism built in, while an easing would support management's own claim that supply constraints are manageable; and (2) a **change in the insider-selling pattern** — the current pattern (broad, multi-executive, one-directional, unconfirmed 10b5-1 status) is currently being discounted by a 0%-Sell sell-side consensus, but either a pause in selling or, more tellingly, any open-market buying by the CEO or other senior officers would be a notable signal in either direction, particularly given it is one of the few points where this analysis diverges from the sell-side commentary reviewed. Consistent with the framework's own guidance, the next routine quarterly earnings print by itself is unlikely to be the catalyst — Q4 FY26 results are expected to land close to (or alongside) the September Investor Briefing, which is precisely what would make that combined event, rather than the earnings print in isolation, the pivotal disclosure to watch.
