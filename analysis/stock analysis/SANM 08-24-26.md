# SANM — Synthesis

**Growth**: Reported growth is huge (+58.6% TTM sales) but overwhelmingly inorganic — the October 2025 ZT Systems acquisition is the majority source of incremental dollars, and that boost rolls out of the YoY comps as the acquisition anniversary passes. The organic "core Sanmina" business underneath is real and itself accelerating (12.6% FY26 guided core growth, ahead of the original high-single-digit target), driven by a genuine AI-infrastructure mix shift, but it's an order of magnitude smaller than the headline consolidated rate. The next leg of growth (the next-gen accelerated-compute program) hasn't even been included in Q4 FY26 guidance and is an early-S-curve bet dependent on AMD's pace and a handful of hyperscaler decisions outside Sanmina's control.

**Profitability**: Gross margin (9.0% TTM) is structurally thin — less than half peer TTMI's 20.7% — for industry reasons (pass-through, materials-heavy EMS assembly in a "highly competitive," capacity-surplus industry) rather than a temporary reinvestment dip, and sits only 1.2pp above its own 5-year trough despite years of growth. FCF is positive on a trailing basis (4.7% of sales TTM) but collapsed to 0.7% of sales in the most recent quarter on deliberate, disclosed working-capital investment ahead of the AI ramp — a credible explanation, but one that leaves near-term cash generation genuinely volatile and pressured further before it eases.

**Risk**: Customer concentration jumped sharply alongside the ZT deal (top-10 now 67% of sales, up from ~52%, with the first-ever single customers over 10%), tied directly to hyperscaler capex cycles the 10-Q says are "largely outside of our control." Debt rose materially to fund the acquisition, though it remains well within covenant limits and liquidity is ample. The clearest accounting-quality flag is a since-remediated FY2023-24 material weakness rooted in "inappropriate tone at the top" affecting contract-cost estimates — resolved on paper but a real mark on management's estimate-setting discipline. Layered on top: management is candid operationally but promotional in framing, and insiders (CEO included) sold $40.44M with zero purchases over the last six months even while publicly raising growth targets.

**Affordability**: The market is split. The formal 18-analyst panel sits at a muted Hold (61.1% Hold, 11.1% Sell), with two institutional initiations (JPMorgan, Susquehanna) carrying price targets below the current price — closer to this analysis's skeptical read. Retail/algorithmic coverage (Zacks, Seeking Alpha) is far more bullish and calls the stock undervalued, pointing to a low 0.83x EV/Sales versus TTMI's 3.45x. The stock is up 171.5% off its 24-month trough but has already pulled back ~29% from a May 2026 peak — a market that swung toward the unqualified bull case and has since partially, not fully, walked it back. The pending FY2027 guide (expected ~late October 2026) is the concrete, scheduled event most likely to force convergence one way or the other.

## How does the company make money?

Sanmina is a contract electronics manufacturer (EMS): customers hand it a product design and Sanmina builds it, from components through finished, tested, shipped goods, plus supply-chain and after-market support (10-K; profile.md). It reports two segments. Integrated Manufacturing Solutions (IMS) — PCB assembly/test, high-level assembly/test, direct order fulfillment — is by far the larger piece, roughly 80-90% of revenue. Components, Products and Services (CPS) is the smaller, higher-margin piece: advanced PCBs, backplanes, cable assemblies, metal fabrication, plus proprietary product lines (Viking memory/storage, SCI defense & aerospace, 42Q manufacturing-execution software) (10-K; numbers.md segmentation).

The business has been transformed by the October 2025 acquisition of ZT Systems' data-center infrastructure manufacturing business, funded with roughly $1.6B of cash/stock/contingent consideration (10-K; 10-Q). ZT builds AI/accelerated-compute racks (largely on AMD platforms) and now supplies the majority of incremental revenue: it contributed $1.1-1.9B/quarter in the two reported quarters since close, versus $2.4-2.8B/quarter for the legacy "core Sanmina" business (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Revenue by end market in Q3 FY26: Communications Networks/Cloud & AI Infrastructure was 62% of revenue ($2.15B, +173% YoY, driven mostly by ZT), and the combined Industrial/Energy/Medical/Defense & Aerospace/Automotive bucket was 38% ($1.32B, +5% YoY) (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Revenue is recognized over time as products are built (95%+ of revenue), reflecting the fact that Sanmina makes bespoke products it can't resell to anyone else (10-K).

## What are its competitive advantages?

Sanmina's own risk-factor disclosures name price as a competitive factor it doesn't always win on — "we may not be able to offer prices as low as some of our competitors for any number of reasons" (10-K) — but this is standard risk-factor boilerplate common to any company in a price-sensitive industry, hedging a general possibility rather than naming a competitor or a specific gap; it shouldn't be read as an admission that Sanmina is priced above peers today. Its IP position is a firmer basis for a similar conclusion: it holds patents on manufacturing processes but says a number "have expired and will continue to expire," directly reducing its ability to assert them against competitors, and it leans more on trade secrets than patents (10-K). R&D spend confirms this isn't an IP-driven business: R&D/Sales is only 0.3-0.4% of revenue, versus 0.9-1.3% at peer TTM Technologies (numbers.md). This is normal for EMS — the model is manufacturing execution, not product invention.

Where the moat, such as it is, actually sits: (1) End-to-end breadth — design, fabrication, assembly, test, logistics, and after-market support under one roof, which the company argues deepens customer relationships and improves cross-sell (10-K). (2) Sticky, long-tenured customer relationships — management cites customers "well over 15 to 20 years," some over 35 years, which raises switching costs once a program is qualified on Sanmina's lines (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md). (3) Global manufacturing footprint (Americas, Europe, Asia) matched to where customers need production, plus specific certifications (FDA for medical, IATF 16949 for automotive, facility security clearance for defense) that are costly for a customer to requalify elsewhere (10-K). (4) A newly acquired strategic position in AI hardware: AMD named Sanmina (via ZT) its "NPI manufacturing partner of choice" for AI rack/cluster-scale systems, and Sanmina has since also picked up work with alternate chip/rack designers like Cerebras (10-Q; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

None of this is a durable, hard-to-replicate moat in the way IP or brand would be, but the actual pricing gap versus peers is less clear-cut than a single margin comparison suggests. No company in this industry discloses per-unit or per-program pricing — EMS contracts are negotiated bid-by-bid and confidential — so gross margin is the best available proxy, and an imperfect one: it conflates markup with cost efficiency, and it excludes component costs, the largest share of what a customer actually pays and the part that's largely commodity-priced across the industry regardless of which EMS provider is used. On that proxy, SANM's TTM gross margin (9.0%, numbers.md) is roughly tied with Jabil's (~9.0-9.2%) and Flex's (~9.3-9.4%), trails Celestica's (~11.5-12.3%) by a real but modest 2.5-3.5pp, and is dramatically below TTM Technologies' (20.7%, numbers.md) — the Jabil, Flex, and Celestica figures come from outside this dataset (Zacks, GuruFocus, and company earnings releases, as of Aug 2026), not from the company's own required data files. TTMI, though, is a PCB fabrication specialist with more design/technical content than a full-scale contract assembler, which makes it the least representative peer of the group for this comparison — the 10-K itself groups Sanmina's real competitive set as Celestica, Flex, Foxconn, Jabil, Plexus, and Benchmark, an industry it calls "highly competitive" with a "surplus of manufacturing capacity" (10-K). Against the peers most similar to Sanmina's actual business — Jabil and Flex — the margin gap is minor to nonexistent; only against Celestica is it clearly real. [INFERRED: 10-K risk factors + numbers.md peer comparison + external peer margin data — Sanmina's edge is closer to relationship/scale/certification lock-in than to price leadership or IP, but the case that it's meaningfully more expensive than its closest peers is weaker than a TTMI-only comparison implies.]

## How and why are sales growing?

Growth is large and currently decelerating quarter-over-quarter even though it's still very high in level. TTM sales are $12.76B, +58.6% YoY. On a quarterly basis: Q1 FY26 +59.0%, Q2 FY26 +102.3% (the acceleration peak), Q3 FY26 +69.7% — a -32.6pp deceleration from Q2's rate (numbers.md).

The dominant driver is inorganic: the ZT Systems acquisition closed October 27, 2025 and is now the majority source of incremental dollars. Q2 FY26 total revenue was $4.01B (ZT contributed $1.88B of it, some pulled forward from 2H); Q3 FY26 total revenue was $3.46B (ZT contributed $1.1B, down sequentially as certain legacy ZT programs wound down) (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Strip ZT out and the underlying "core Sanmina" business is growing at a much more modest, but still healthy and accelerating, organic rate: +7.3% YoY in Q2 FY26, +17% YoY in Q3 FY26, with full-year FY26 core growth now guided to 12.6% — ahead of the "high-single-digit" target set at the start of the year (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Within core, the Communications Networks & Cloud/AI Infrastructure end market — even excluding ZT — grew 33% in Q3, an acceleration from a roughly 20%/quarter trend over the prior six-to-seven quarters, which management attributes to new customer/program wins tied to the broader AI infrastructure buildout (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). CPS revenue also accelerated, +29% YoY in Q3, driven by metal fabrication for AI system racks and high-tech PCBs for aerospace/defense (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

So: growth is **not organic** in the main — the headline 59-102% YoY figures are overwhelmingly the ZT acquisition showing up in the comps, and that acquisition-driven boost will roll out of the YoY comparison as the anniversary passes. The organic story underneath (low-to-high teens core growth, an AI-infrastructure-driven mix shift within core) is real and itself accelerating, but is an order of magnitude smaller than the reported consolidated growth rate. [INFERRED: numbers.md quarterly table + earnings-call segment commentary — the TTM/quarterly growth and acceleration figures in numbers.md are consolidated and not adjusted for the ZT acquisition, so they overstate organic momentum in a way analogous to the acquisition-driven "growth artifact" pattern common in M&A comps.]

One more thing worth separating out from the ZT story: the filings disclose end-market revenue in only two buckets — "Communications Networks and Cloud [and AI] Infrastructure" versus a single bundled "Industrial, Medical, Defense and Aerospace, and Automotive" line (10-K; 10-Q) — and that bundled, non-AI base is not shrinking. It was $5.39B in FY2023, $4.92B in FY2024, $5.02B in FY2025, and $3.78B for the first nine months of FY2026 — essentially flat versus $3.78B in the same nine months a year earlier (+0.2% YoY) (10-K; 10-Q). What's changed is its *share*: from roughly 60-65% of revenue in FY2023-FY2025 down to 35.5% for the nine months of FY2026, purely because Communications/Cloud/AI grew +205% YoY around it (10-K; 10-Q). [ESTIMATED: percentages calculated from the 10-K's and 10-Q's Note 4 revenue-disaggregation dollar figures — bundled segment revenue ÷ total revenue, by period.] So there is a real, stable, several-billion-dollar diversified base outside AI — it just isn't the growth story, and its relative importance to the business has fallen sharply even though its absolute size hasn't.

## Is there a reasonable expectation of accelerated or continued future sales growth?

**Sector**: Yes — Sanmina now sits directly in the AI/accelerated-compute infrastructure buildout, a sector both management and the 10-K describe as high-growth, but the 10-K also flags that long-term hyperscaler capex is "difficult to predict and largely outside of our control" (10-Q risk factors).

**S-curve stage**: Early. Management is explicit that the *next-generation* accelerated-compute program — the one meant to carry FY27 growth — has not even been included in the Q4 FY26 guide due to revenue-recognition timing, and is expected to only begin contributing in Q1 FY27 and "ramp over time" (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). The FY26 ZT run-rate ($5-6B annualized) is described by the CFO as "not the best annualized run rate to look at" because it's a transition year — legacy accelerated-compute revenue went to roughly zero while the new program is still pre-production (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). [INFERRED: earnings-call commentary — this points to Sanmina being in the early ramp phase of the AI-hardware S-curve, with the bulk of the opportunity still ahead rather than already captured.]

**Sector-wide caution, with an unresolved timeline**: `Sectors.md` frames the AI-hardware S-curve overall as "Mid-Late acceleration," heading toward a "commoditized" phase that favors low-cost producers over today's innovation-premium environment — a real tension with SANM's own "best still ahead" framing, since SANM is explicitly not the low-cost producer (see gross-margin answer). But that thesis is directional, not dated: commoditization follows a shift to abundant supply, and this data set currently shows the opposite — management describes ongoing memory and custom-ASIC shortages likely persisting into FY27, with no margin compression built into that guide (6-7% op margin, CPS guided back to mid-teens). [INFERRED: Sectors.md's commoditization thesis vs. SANM's own supply-shortage and margin commentary — the precondition for commoditization isn't visible in the data yet, so this is a genuine longer-horizon overhang on durability rather than a signal to expect margin compression within the next year; the timing (a year, several years, longer) can't be pinned down from what's disclosed here.]

**Market share**: Not disclosed in a way that lets this be answered with real numbers — this is a gap. Management's own answer to a direct analyst question on AMD/Helios share was that it's "pretty tough to gauge," and the stated ambition is only to "win every one of these large hyperscalers over the next 12 to 18 months," implying share today is still low/building (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md).

**Durable moat**: Weak-to-moderate, per the competitive-advantages answer above — relationship/certification lock-in and the AMD manufacturing-partner-of-choice position are real, but this is a scale/execution business in a competitive, capacity-surplus industry, not one with pricing power or IP protection (10-K). One partial buffer worth noting: `Sectors.md`'s observation that rising profits in one AI-hardware layer squeeze the layer below it (memory up → AMD/Nvidia margins down) extends loosely to SANM as AMD's manufacturing partner, but SANM's contract-manufacturing model largely passes component costs through to customers rather than owning that price risk (10-K) — which should dampen, not eliminate, how much a component-cost squeeze on AMD actually transmits to SANM's own margin.

**Investment in growth**: Clearly yes and accelerating. Capex was $57M in Q2, $101M in Q3, and is guided to $135M in Q4 — funding metal-fabrication capacity for AI racks, new high-layer-count PCB capacity, medium-voltage transformer capacity, and ZT-side power/liquid-cooling/test-cell/automation investment (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

**Leveraging AI**: Directly and structurally — Sanmina's growth engine now *is* AI hardware manufacturing (ZT's accelerated-compute racks), plus its 42Q software offers cloud-based manufacturing execution (10-K; excerpts).

**Growth potential outside AI**: The non-AI end markets aren't just a stable floor — management points to aerospace/defense specifically as a distinct growth avenue, though it can't be sized from this data. Sanmina's SCI subsidiary has served defense/aerospace for "more than 60 years," building aircraft systems, tactical communications, unmanned and counter-unmanned aerial systems, fiber-optics, missile control and guidance systems, and satellite control and communication systems, and holds the facility security clearance the work requires (10-K; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Management describes "expanding customer base in the satellite market" and expects "growth in traditional U.S. Defense & Aerospace business to continue" into FY27/28 (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md), and CPS's high-technology PCB business for aerospace/defense was cited as a specific driver of CPS's +29% YoY growth in Q3 (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). This is a real, named growth thesis, not diversification for its own sake — but it comes with a hard data limit: customers are described only generically as "U.S. government agencies, U.S. allies and major defense and aerospace prime contractors" (10-K), with no company ever named, and defense/aerospace revenue is never broken out on its own — it's permanently bundled with industrial, medical, and automotive in every disclosure (10-K; 10-Q). There's no way, from this data, to size the opportunity or verify the growth claim against an actual number.

**Management commitment**: High. Leadership has raised its FY27 target twice (from "double the business in three years" to "$16B+", now "more confident than ever") and describes itself as personally "very excited" about the trajectory (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

**Challenges**: (1) Customer concentration has risen sharply alongside this growth — top-10 customers were ~50-52% of sales historically but are now 67%, and the 10-Q flags a single customer at 10%+ of sales for the first time in the periods shown, directly tied to hyperscaler dependence (10-Q). (2) Component shortages (memory, custom ASICs) are constraining how much Sanmina can actually ship — management called this an ongoing constraint likely persisting into FY27 (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). (3) ZT's revenue mix is lumpy quarter to quarter as legacy programs roll off before the next-gen program ramps in (Q4 guide for ZT revenue is *below* what was implied last quarter, purely from legacy-program timing) (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). (4) Pace is partly out of Sanmina's hands — it depends on AMD's own "measured approach" to ramping customer manufacturing partners (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md, analyst question referencing AMD commentary).

## Is it highly dependent on a small number of customers?

Yes, and the dependency has gotten meaningfully worse. Sales to the ten largest customers were approximately 50-52% of net sales historically (52% in FY2025, and the 10-K describes "approximately half" as the longstanding pattern), but that concentration jumped to 67% of net sales in the most recent quarter (10-K; 10-Q). For the first time in the periods disclosed, single customers crossed the 10% threshold: one customer was 10%+ of net sales for the three months ended June 27, 2026, and two customers were 10%+ for the nine months ended — versus "no customer represented 10% or more" in the comparable prior-year periods (10-Q).

The company attributes this directly to the ZT Systems acquisition: "our dependence on the cloud and AI infrastructure market has significantly increased. This market is subject to rapid technological shifts and demand is heavily influenced by the capital expenditure cycles of a small number of hyperscale cloud providers" (10-Q risk factors). Management's own framing reinforces this — the strategic goal is explicitly to "win every one of these large hyperscalers over the next 12 to 18 months" (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md), meaning the business is being built around deepening ties to a handful of hyperscaler accounts, not diversifying away from them. On the other side, contracts don't carry firm volume commitments — customers "usually do not make firm orders for product delivery more than thirty to ninety days in advance" and can cancel or reschedule (10-K) — so revenue tied to any one large customer is not contractually locked in even during the buildout. [INFERRED: 10-K/10-Q customer-concentration disclosures — the diversification Sanmina has across end markets (communications, industrial, medical, defense, automotive) does not offset customer concentration, since the fastest-growing end market (cloud/AI infrastructure) is itself now concentrated in a small number of hyperscaler and OEM accounts.]

## Does it have a worthwhile gross margin? If not, is there a good reason why the margin is low?

The margin is thin in absolute terms, and roughly in line with — not dramatically behind — its closest EMS peers, though well behind TTM Technologies, a differently-positioned PCB specialist (see the competitive-advantages answer above for the fuller peer comparison and its caveats). TTM gross margin is 9.0% (numbers.md), essentially tied with Jabil and Flex, trailing Celestica by 2.5-3.5pp, and well below TTMI's 20.7% (numbers.md; external sources for Jabil/Flex/Celestica). It is also barely above its own 5-year trough: 9.0% TTM vs. a 7.8% trough (FY2021), a cushion of only 1.2pp, even after 5 years of AI-driven growth and a transformative acquisition (numbers.md). The 5-year trend has been a slow, steady climb (7.8% → 7.9% → 8.3% → 8.5% → 8.8% → 9.0% TTM), not a sharp re-rating.

The reason is structural, not a temporary reinvestment dip. IMS — 80-90% of revenue — is a pass-through, materials-heavy assembly business: Sanmina buys components on customers' behalf and marks up primarily for labor, overhead, and logistics, in an industry the 10-K itself calls "highly competitive" with a "surplus of manufacturing capacity" (10-K). This is a structurally low-margin business model shared across the EMS peer group (see above), not evidence that Sanmina specifically prices above or executes worse than its closest peers — the 10-K's price-competition language ("we may not be able to offer prices as low as some of our competitors") is standard risk-factor boilerplate rather than a specific admission (10-K). Sanmina competes on relationships and scale, not on price leadership or proprietary technology, and its margin reflects the industry it's in more than a company-specific weakness. It is not primarily a low-margin-because-of-growth-investment story: R&D/Sales is only 0.3% of revenue (numbers.md), far too small to be depressing gross margin.

There is a real margin story within the mix, though. IMS gross margin actually improved to 10.2% in Q3 FY26 (up 270bps YoY), which management attributes to the ZT Systems business itself plus favorable mix and one-time non-recurring engineering services (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md) — meaning ZT, so far, is a net positive to blended margin, not a drag. CPS, the higher-margin segment (13.9% in FY2025 vs. IMS's 7.7%), has seen margin *compress* recently (12.8% in Q3 FY26, down 190bps YoY) because of depreciation on new AI-rack and PCB capacity investment — management explicitly frames this as a temporary reinvestment effect and guides CPS back "above the mid-teens" once new capacity fills up (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md; SANM_10q.txt). So: the *low* overall margin is structural and industry-driven; the *recent softness in CPS specifically* is a genuine reinvestment story, but CPS is only ~20% of revenue, so it cannot lift the consolidated number much on its own. Management's own longer-term target for total-company non-GAAP operating margin is a modest 6-7% (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md) — this is not a business guiding toward high profitability.

## Is it generating FCF? If not, is there a good reason why?

Yes on a trailing basis, but the quarterly trend is deteriorating sharply and the "good reason" is a real one: working-capital consumption to fund the AI ramp. TTM FCF/Sales is 4.7%, well above the 5-year trough of 0.5% (FY2023) and comfortably ahead of peer TTMI, which is currently FCF-negative (-0.4% FCF/Sales TTM) (numbers.md). That TTM figure, though, is propped up by a very strong Q2 FY26 ($342M FCF, 8.5% of sales) that has since reversed hard: Q3 FY26 free cash flow was just $23.6M, or 0.7% of sales — a 93% sequential drop — on operating cash flow of only $124.5M, itself down 69% from Q2's $399M (numbers.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

Management's explanation is explicit and consistent quarter to quarter: working capital is building ahead of the next-generation accelerated-compute ramp, and this is deliberate. Inventory was up 87.2% YoY in Q3 (net of customer advances), and the CFO told analysts directly: "we do expect working capital to build; that is an investment in the business... I do expect some pressure on our overall working capital numbers. But again, that is a positive thing" (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). CapEx is also rising steadily — $57M (Q2) → $101M (Q3) → $135M guided (Q4) — funding metal-fab, PCB, transformer, and ZT power/cooling/test-cell capacity (numbers.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). This is a credible explanation, consistent with the sales-growth and capex answers in Pass 1, and it is disclosed transparently rather than buried. The caveat: it does mean FCF in any single recent quarter is a poor read of underlying cash generation, and the near-term trajectory (working capital "will start to accelerate" per the CFO) points to further FCF pressure before it eases, not immediate relief (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

## Are liabilities and expenses reasonable and manageable?

Debt increased sharply to fund the ZT acquisition, but current coverage and liquidity look adequate, with one caveat about how leverage is measured. Total debt jumped from $0.39B (FY2025) to $2.43B TTM, driven by $1.4B drawn on Term Loan A and $0.8B on Term Loan B at the ZT closing (10-K). Debt/Sales rose to 19.1% TTM — still below TTMI's 31.9% — but Debt/OCF, at 2.70x TTM, has been extremely volatile quarter to quarter (14.01x in Q1 FY26, 5.45x in Q2, 19.54x in Q3) purely because OCF itself swings so much with working-capital timing (numbers.md). Interest coverage fell from an unusually high 80.0x in FY2025 (a low-debt year) to 5.9x TTM, with quarterly readings as low as 3.5x (Q1 FY26) (numbers.md) — still above the credit agreement's minimum 3.00x interest-coverage covenant, but with less room than the TTM figure alone suggests. The company's own leverage metric — net leverage ratio, calculated off annualized EBITDA rather than OCF — was 0.29x as of Q3 FY26, far below the 4.00x maximum covenant, and management states it was in compliance with all covenants (10-Q; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). [INFERRED: numbers.md vs. 10-Q covenant disclosures — the EBITDA-based leverage ratio the company reports and the OCF-based Debt/OCF ratio in numbers.md tell different stories (very comfortable vs. volatile) because EBITDA smooths out the working-capital swings that make quarterly OCF lumpy; both are legitimate lenses, but the covenant that actually governs default risk is the EBITDA-based one, and on that measure leverage is low.] Liquidity is ample regardless: ~$4B available including cash and undrawn revolver/delayed-draw capacity, with no revolver borrowings outstanding (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Maturities are not a near-term concern — Term Loan A matures 2030, Term Loan B matures 2032, with modest scheduled amortization in the early years (10-Q).

Other expense items are unremarkable. SBC/Sales is a modest 0.7% TTM, drifting up slowly from 0.5% five years ago (numbers.md) — a real but small cost, not a red flag. SG&A/Sales is stable around 3.2-3.6% (numbers.md). CapEx is the standout: TTM capex of $0.31B is more than double the 5-year average ($0.13B), and CapEx/D&A of 186.6% TTM means the company is investing well ahead of its depreciation run-rate (numbers.md) — this is the AI-capacity buildout discussed above, a real and rising expense, but one directly tied to the growth thesis rather than waste. There is also a capped, disclosed contingent liability from the ZT deal — up to $450M in earnout payments over three years, carried at an estimated $183M fair value (10-Q) — plus routine litigation/environmental reserves ($48M) that are small relative to the balance sheet (10-Q). Nothing here looks unmanageable; the main thing to watch is that leverage and cash coverage numbers will look noisy quarter to quarter until the ZT ramp and its working-capital needs stabilize.

## Do accounting choices appear to be inflating or depressing reported earnings?

**Earnings vs. cash (master check):** No sign of earnings being inflated relative to cash — if anything the opposite. OCF/Net Income is 2.92x TTM, up sharply from 1.36x five years ago (numbers.md), meaning reported profit converts into more cash than it represents, not less — a reassuring signal on its face. But the quarterly figures are erratic (3.63x, 4.26x, then 1.06x in Q3 FY26 — a 75% sequential drop) (numbers.md), which tracks the same working-capital swings discussed in the FCF answer rather than a deterioration in earnings quality; still, it means no single quarter's OCF/NI ratio should be trusted as a read on underlying quality.

**Revenue quality:** Revenue recognition is largely mechanical and disclosed as standard for the industry — 95%+ of revenue is recognized over time because Sanmina's products are bespoke and have no alternative use (10-K), which is a reasonable, conservative basis (it ties revenue to actual production progress, not shipment gamesmanship). The one area flagged as judgment-heavy by Sanmina's own auditor is the cost-to-cost method used for the SCI defense/aerospace division's government contracts — PwC identified this as a "critical audit matter" specifically because of "the significant judgment by management when developing the total estimated costs at completion" (10-K). This is worth flagging as a place where management discretion has real earnings impact, though it's disclosed rather than hidden, and SCI is a small piece of the business.

**Reserves and timing / governance — the one real red flag:** Sanmina disclosed material weaknesses in internal controls for fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024, and the root cause named is specifically management-driven: "inappropriate tone at the top" at one division led to "inappropriate and unsupported adjustments to the quarterly contract cost estimate process," alongside a separate weakness in accounting for transfer of control of raw-materials inventory to customers (10-K). Both were remediated as of the end of FY2025 per management's own assessment, with the controls tested and operating effectively (10-K). This is meaningfully different from a routine technical restatement — it describes management overriding or manipulating the estimates that directly feed reported segment income. It predates the current fiscal year and appears resolved, but it belongs in the record.

**Non-GAAP and SBC:** All of management's commentary on the earnings calls is explicitly non-GAAP (excludes SBC, amortization, restructuring, and acquisition/integration costs) (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md) — standard practice, but it means headline "8% operating margin" language is not the GAAP number. SBC itself is small (0.7% of sales) so the add-back doesn't swing the picture much. Acquisition and integration charges were $34M in FY2025, tied specifically to ZT — a genuinely one-time cost category for now, worth re-checking if it recurs into FY2027.

**Tax:** The effective tax rate moved in a way that *depressed*, not inflated, recent GAAP earnings — it rose to 35% in Q3 FY26 and 29% for the nine months, versus 19-20% in the comparable prior-year periods, driven by non-deductible acquisition-related charges and the closing of a legacy fiscal 2008-2010 IRS audit (10-Q). The prior year (FY2025) told the opposite small story: the tax rate fell to 22% from 25%, "primarily due to the release of tax reserves" (10-K) — a modest, disclosed one-off benefit, not a large one.

**Goodwill and purchase accounting:** ZT purchase accounting is not finalized — goodwill has been revised down twice already as working-capital and valuation estimates firm up (a $185M-plus cumulative reduction through the nine months) (10-Q), and the company states outright that "the initial accounting for the ZT Acquisition is incomplete" (10-K). This is normal for a deal this size this recently, not a red flag by itself, but it means goodwill and intangible balances (and therefore future amortization/impairment risk) are still moving targets.

**Net assessment:** No evidence of revenue manipulation, reserve smoothing, or aggressive capitalization. The clearest, most relevant finding is the FY2023-24 tone-at-the-top material weakness around contract-cost estimates — now remediated on paper, but a genuine mark against the reliability of management's own internal estimate-setting in the recent past, and it directly informs the trustworthiness question below.

## Is management trustworthy?

Mixed. On operating transparency, management is reasonably candid: on the Q3 call the CFO volunteers that market share is "pretty tough to gauge," walks through exactly how much of the margin beat came from non-recurring engineering services versus core mix, and is explicit that the ZT $5-6B "run rate" figure is not representative because FY2026 is a transition year (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). That is a level of specificity that goes beyond boilerplate. Tone has not shifted between the two calls in this data set — both Q2 and Q3 FY26 calls are consistently upbeat and promotional in framing ("very pleased," "very excited," "this is a different company than a year ago," "personally very excited about our future") (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md); there's no detectable change in confidence, positive or negative, but the framing throughout leans promotional rather than neutral.

Background adds some relevant context, though it sits outside this dataset's required files: CEO Jure Sola is Sanmina's actual founder, having started the company in 1980 and served as chairman continuously since 1991 (external source: company investor communications, Crunchbase). His CEO tenure hasn't been continuous, though — he stepped down to Executive Chairman in 2017 when the board brought in an outside CEO (Bob Eulau), who was succeeded by a second outside CEO (Hartmut Liebel), before Sola returned as CEO in August 2020 when Liebel departed (external source: Sanmina 8-K filings, 2017 and 2020). That cuts both ways: 45+ years of founder-level alignment with the business is a genuine long-run trust signal, but two outside CEOs each lasting only a few years before the board went back to the (now 74-75-year-old) founder is a thin bench and doesn't say much for succession planning at a company currently navigating its biggest transformation. Neither point is decisive alone, and both are offered as outside context rather than as findings grounded in the required data files.

Insider trading is the strongest signal here, and it points the wrong way: open-market activity over the last six months was $0 bought (zero purchases) against $40.44M sold across 15 separate sales (SANM_management.md) — CEO Jure Sola sold $27.09M in a single transaction (May 6), CFO Jonathan Faust sold roughly $2.7M across two sales, EVP Alan McWilliams Reid sold in four separate smaller transactions, and two directors and the chief accounting officer also sold (SANM_management.md; SANM_news.md). This selling happened while the stock roughly tripled off its 24-month trough ($68.45 → a 2026 high near $260) (numbers.md) — consistent with normal profit-taking after a large run-up, and at least one CFO sale was disclosed as executed under a pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 plan, which reduces (but doesn't eliminate) concern about opportunistic timing (SANM_news.md). Still, the complete absence of any insider buying, from a leadership team that is simultaneously telling the market it is "more confident than ever" in a $16B+ FY2027 target, is a real tension worth weighing — management is monetizing conviction, not adding to it.

The FY2023-24 material weakness discussed above also bears directly on this question: it was not an isolated technical error but a described breakdown in "tone at the top" at one division that led to unsupported adjustments to cost estimates feeding reported results (10-K). It is now remediated per management's own testing, and the auditor (PwC, engaged since 2016) has not flagged an ongoing issue, but it is recent enough, and specific enough to management conduct rather than a system glitch, to weigh against a clean bill of trust. Litigation history (an SCI/defense False Claims Act qui tam suit settled for $5.9M without admission of liability, several wage-and-hour class/PAGA suits in California, legacy environmental remediation at multiple sites) (10-K; 10-Q) reads as ordinary operational and regulatory friction for a company of this size and footprint, not evidence of dishonesty, and doesn't move the assessment much either way.

**Net:** No evidence of ongoing fraud or deception, and disclosure/communication quality on operating specifics is good. But the combination of one-way insider selling during the exact window management is promoting its best-ever growth story, plus a recent (if resolved) management-level control failure over the company's own cost estimates, is enough to withhold full confidence — trustworthy on disclosure, but the money and the words aren't fully aligned.

## What is the financial community's current appraisal of the company and its growth prospects?

The appraisal splits sharply by audience, and that split is itself the headline finding. The formal 18-analyst panel sits at a muted **Hold** consensus: 0 Strong Buy, 5 Buy (27.8%), 11 Hold (61.1%), 2 Sell (11.1%), 0 Strong Sell (SANM_analyst.md) — not a bearish call, but far short of the enthusiasm the growth headline (+58.6% TTM sales) might suggest. Two of the more institutional names in the coverage list, JPMorgan and Susquehanna, initiated coverage at Neutral with price targets of $145 and $135 respectively (SANM_news.md) — both *below* the current price of $185.86 (numbers.md), meaning their "neutral" is functionally a call for downside, not just "wait and see." Grade movement in the last 90 days shows only "1 maintained," with no tracked upgrades, downgrades, or initiations in that window (SANM_analyst.md).

Retail- and algorithm-facing coverage (Zacks, Wall Street Zen, Seeking Alpha) tells a much more bullish story that runs concurrently in the same window: Zacks Rank #1 "Strong Buy" placements repeatedly through May-August, a Wall Street Zen "strong-buy" upgrade (Aug 1), Seeking Alpha reiterating Strong Buy with a $277 target (Aug 4) and an earlier $312 target (Jun 25) citing "14% base case upside and up to 45% in the bull scenario," and an Aug 24 headline asking "Are Investors Undervaluing Sanmina Right Now?" (SANM_news.md). [INFERRED: comparing SANM_analyst.md's "no upgrades in 90 days" against SANM_news.md's Aug 1 Wall Street Zen upgrade — these two sources appear to track different, non-overlapping analyst/rating panels (a smaller set of traditional sell-side firms vs. broader retail-rating aggregators), which is why they disagree on recent grade activity; the gap itself is a useful signal that the "bullish" coverage skews retail/algorithmic rather than institutional.]

## Is the financial community's appraisal affecting the company's fundamentals?

Only modest evidence either way; this is a data gap rather than a clean answer. There is no sign the current elevated price has been used to opportunistically raise capital: the ZT acquisition, signed in May 2025 before the bulk of the run-up, was funded mostly with debt and cash, with only $150M of stock issued as consideration (10-K) — a small, fixed-share issuance rather than an opportunistic equity raise timed to a high price. The Board did authorize an *additional* $600M of share buybacks in Q3 FY26 on top of the existing $600M program (SANM_10q.txt; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md), but management frames repurchases as routinely offsetting dilution from equity compensation, not as a reaction to price ("we repurchased approximately 1.1 million shares... to offset the remaining dilution for the year," SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md) — no explicit signal that the buyback pace is being timed off perceived under- or over-valuation.

There's also no evidence the price move (in either direction) has pressured operating decisions — capex and working-capital investment have scaled steadily with the ZT ramp regardless of the stock's swings, and liquidity (~$4B available) means the company isn't dependent on market access to fund near-term growth (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). The clearest place sentiment does touch fundamentals is more indirect: heavy, broad institutional buying (Swiss National Bank, Invesco, Vaughan Nelson, Geode, Norges Bank all reported adding positions through Q2-Q3 FY26) (SANM_news.md) sits alongside the insider selling documented in Pass 2 — a classic pattern of institutions chasing the growth narrative while executives who have direct visibility into execution risk are net sellers, but the data here doesn't show that flowing back into a fundamentals effect (e.g., no evidence of the company using investor enthusiasm to push customer contract terms or accelerate spending beyond what's operationally planned).

## How does the financial community's appraisal differ from our analysis? Which perspective is reflected in the price?

Our analysis (Passes 1-2) found: growth is overwhelmingly acquisition-driven, not organic, even though the organic core (low-to-high-teens) is genuinely healthy; gross margin is structurally thin and only 1.2pp above a 5-year trough, for industry-structural reasons rather than a temporary dip; FCF is real on a trailing basis but collapsing quarter to quarter on deliberate working-capital investment; customer concentration has jumped sharply (67% top-10, first-ever 10%+ single customers); and management shows a real tension between promotional public messaging and one-way, zero-purchase insider selling, alongside a recent (if remediated) accounting-control weakness.

The retail/algorithmic coverage (Zacks, Seeking Alpha, Wall Street Zen) largely takes the headline growth and margin-expansion story at face value and calls the stock undervalued — it does not appear to net out the ZT-acquisition effect from organic growth, nor does it weight the insider-selling or FY23-24 material-weakness findings from this analysis. The formal 18-analyst panel's Hold consensus, and in particular JPMorgan's and Susquehanna's Neutral initiations with targets *below* the current price, land much closer to our skeptical read — professional coverage appears to already be pricing in some of the durability and margin-structure concerns this analysis surfaced, even without necessarily itemizing the acquisition-vs-organic growth split explicitly.

The price itself reflects a partial, not full, convergence toward skepticism: EV/Sales of 0.83x is unusually cheap for the reported growth rate, which is consistent with the market implicitly discounting the "growth is mostly inorganic and thin-margin" reality rather than paying up for the headline percentage — in that specific sense, the price already leans toward our view more than the loudest retail commentary does. But the 171.5% run off the 24-month trough, and the fact that the stock still traded as high as $259 before its recent pullback, shows the market did, for a period, price in something closer to the unqualified bull case; the current ~$185 level reads as a partial retreat from that peak enthusiasm rather than a level that had fully absorbed the concentration, margin, and cash-flow-volatility findings from this analysis. ## What catalyst(s) may force the financial community's appraisal to converge with ours?

**The formal FY2027 guide, expected at the Q4 FY26 earnings call (~90 days after the Q3 call, so around late October 2026).** This is the specific, scheduled disclosure event both management and analysts are waiting on — not a routine earnings print. Management explicitly deferred the details: "it is still too early to formally guide fiscal year 2027... we will talk more about it in 90 days" (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). This guide will need to specify: (1) whether the next-generation accelerated-compute program (deliberately excluded from the Q4 FY26 outlook on revenue-recognition timing) actually begins contributing in Q1 FY27 as promised; (2) the margin profile as ZT-related AI revenue becomes a larger share of the mix, a question analysts pressed on directly in Q3 (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md); and (3) whether working capital and FCF start normalizing or continue deteriorating as the ramp proceeds. Any of these landing short of the "$16B+ and more confident than ever" framing would be a concrete, checkable data point, not just a routine beat/miss.

**The company's own next 1-2 quarters of FCF and working-capital data**, which will directly test the Pass 2 finding that the Q3 FY26 FCF collapse was temporary ramp-related investment rather than a structural deterioration — the CFO has already flagged that working capital "will start to accelerate" further (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md), so the near-term data should make clear whether this normalizes on the timeline management describes.

**A concrete AMD/Helios production-share disclosure.** Management currently cannot (or will not) quantify Sanmina's share of AMD's ramp ("it is pretty tough to gauge," SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md); AMD itself has signaled a "measured approach" to ramping manufacturing partners (referenced in an analyst question, SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Either a specific share/volume disclosure from Sanmina or from AMD directly would let the market re-underwrite the size of Sanmina's addressable piece of the AI buildout, which is currently more assumed than demonstrated.

**The "Nvidia-driven revenue catalyst" flagged for September 2026** in earlier bull-case commentary (SANM_news.md, Jun 2026 Seeking Alpha piece) — outside this data set's visibility as of the analysis date, but a specific, named, near-term event worth tracking rather than a vague "AI tailwind" reference.

Analysts and management are focused on the same short list — margin trajectory as mix shifts, working-capital/FCF normalization, and the formal FY2027 guide — which suggests the market already knows where the convergence-or-divergence test will happen; it just hasn't happened yet.
