## How does the company make money?

Sanmina is a contract electronics manufacturer (EMS): customers hand it a product design and Sanmina builds it, from components through finished, tested, shipped goods, plus supply-chain and after-market support (10-K; profile.md). It reports two segments. Integrated Manufacturing Solutions (IMS) — PCB assembly/test, high-level assembly/test, direct order fulfillment — is by far the larger piece, roughly 80-90% of revenue. Components, Products and Services (CPS) is the smaller, higher-margin piece: advanced PCBs, backplanes, cable assemblies, metal fabrication, plus proprietary product lines (Viking memory/storage, SCI defense & aerospace, 42Q manufacturing-execution software) (10-K; numbers.md segmentation).

The business has been transformed by the October 2025 acquisition of ZT Systems' data-center infrastructure manufacturing business, funded with roughly $1.6B of cash/stock/contingent consideration (10-K; 10-Q). ZT builds AI/accelerated-compute racks (largely on AMD platforms) and now supplies the majority of incremental revenue: it contributed $1.1-1.9B/quarter in the two reported quarters since close, versus $2.4-2.8B/quarter for the legacy "core Sanmina" business (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Revenue by end market in Q3 FY26: Communications Networks/Cloud & AI Infrastructure was 62% of revenue ($2.15B, +173% YoY, driven mostly by ZT), and the combined Industrial/Energy/Medical/Defense & Aerospace/Automotive bucket was 38% ($1.32B, +5% YoY) (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Revenue is recognized over time as products are built (95%+ of revenue), reflecting the fact that Sanmina makes bespoke products it can't resell to anyone else (10-K).

## What are its competitive advantages?

Sanmina is explicit that it does not compete primarily on being the low-cost or low-price producer — the 10-K states it "may not be able to offer prices as low as some of our competitors" (10-K). Its IP position is similarly modest: it holds patents on manufacturing processes but says a number "have expired and will continue to expire," directly reducing its ability to assert them against competitors, and it leans more on trade secrets than patents (10-K). R&D spend confirms this isn't an IP-driven business: R&D/Sales is only 0.3-0.4% of revenue, versus 0.9-1.3% at peer TTM Technologies (numbers.md). This is normal for EMS — the model is manufacturing execution, not product invention.

Where the moat, such as it is, actually sits: (1) End-to-end breadth — design, fabrication, assembly, test, logistics, and after-market support under one roof, which the company argues deepens customer relationships and improves cross-sell (10-K). (2) Sticky, long-tenured customer relationships — management cites customers "well over 15 to 20 years," some over 35 years, which raises switching costs once a program is qualified on Sanmina's lines (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md). (3) Global manufacturing footprint (Americas, Europe, Asia) matched to where customers need production, plus specific certifications (FDA for medical, IATF 16949 for automotive, facility security clearance for defense) that are costly for a customer to requalify elsewhere (10-K). (4) A newly acquired strategic position in AI hardware: AMD named Sanmina (via ZT) its "NPI manufacturing partner of choice" for AI rack/cluster-scale systems, and Sanmina has since also picked up work with alternate chip/rack designers like Cerebras (10-Q; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

None of this is a durable, hard-to-replicate moat in the way IP or brand would be — peer gross margin comparison makes the point starkly: SANM's 9.0% gross margin is well below TTMI's 20.7% (numbers.md), and the 10-K itself calls the EMS industry "highly competitive" with a "surplus of manufacturing capacity" among named rivals (Celestica, Flex, Foxconn, Jabil, Plexus, Benchmark) (10-K). ## How and why are sales growing?

Growth is large and currently decelerating quarter-over-quarter even though it's still very high in level. TTM sales are $12.76B, +58.6% YoY. On a quarterly basis: Q1 FY26 +59.0%, Q2 FY26 +102.3% (the acceleration peak), Q3 FY26 +69.7% — a -32.6pp deceleration from Q2's rate (numbers.md).

The dominant driver is inorganic: the ZT Systems acquisition closed October 27, 2025 and is now the majority source of incremental dollars. Q2 FY26 total revenue was $4.01B (ZT contributed $1.88B of it, some pulled forward from 2H); Q3 FY26 total revenue was $3.46B (ZT contributed $1.1B, down sequentially as certain legacy ZT programs wound down) (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Strip ZT out and the underlying "core Sanmina" business is growing at a much more modest, but still healthy and accelerating, organic rate: +7.3% YoY in Q2 FY26, +17% YoY in Q3 FY26, with full-year FY26 core growth now guided to 12.6% — ahead of the "high-single-digit" target set at the start of the year (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). Within core, the Communications Networks & Cloud/AI Infrastructure end market — even excluding ZT — grew 33% in Q3, an acceleration from a roughly 20%/quarter trend over the prior six-to-seven quarters, which management attributes to new customer/program wins tied to the broader AI infrastructure buildout (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). CPS revenue also accelerated, +29% YoY in Q3, driven by metal fabrication for AI system racks and high-tech PCBs for aerospace/defense (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

So: growth is **not organic** in the main — the headline 59-102% YoY figures are overwhelmingly the ZT acquisition showing up in the comps, and that acquisition-driven boost will roll out of the YoY comparison as the anniversary passes. The organic story underneath (low-to-high teens core growth, an AI-infrastructure-driven mix shift within core) is real and itself accelerating, but is an order of magnitude smaller than the reported consolidated growth rate. ## Is there a reasonable expectation of accelerated or continued future sales growth?

**Sector**: Yes — Sanmina now sits directly in the AI/accelerated-compute infrastructure buildout, a sector both management and the 10-K describe as high-growth, but the 10-K also flags that long-term hyperscaler capex is "difficult to predict and largely outside of our control" (10-Q risk factors).

**S-curve stage**: Early. Management is explicit that the *next-generation* accelerated-compute program — the one meant to carry FY27 growth — has not even been included in the Q4 FY26 guide due to revenue-recognition timing, and is expected to only begin contributing in Q1 FY27 and "ramp over time" (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). The FY26 ZT run-rate ($5-6B annualized) is described by the CFO as "not the best annualized run rate to look at" because it's a transition year — legacy accelerated-compute revenue went to roughly zero while the new program is still pre-production (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). [INFERRED: earnings-call commentary — this points to Sanmina being in the early ramp phase of the AI-hardware S-curve, with the bulk of the opportunity still ahead rather than already captured.]

**Market share**: Not disclosed in a way that lets this be answered with real numbers — this is a gap. Management's own answer to a direct analyst question on AMD/Helios share was that it's "pretty tough to gauge," and the stated ambition is only to "win every one of these large hyperscalers over the next 12 to 18 months," implying share today is still low/building (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md).

**Durable moat**: Weak-to-moderate, per the competitive-advantages answer above — relationship/certification lock-in and the AMD manufacturing-partner-of-choice position are real, but this is a scale/execution business in a competitive, capacity-surplus industry, not one with pricing power or IP protection (10-K).

**Investment in growth**: Clearly yes and accelerating. Capex was $57M in Q2, $101M in Q3, and is guided to $135M in Q4 — funding metal-fabrication capacity for AI racks, new high-layer-count PCB capacity, medium-voltage transformer capacity, and ZT-side power/liquid-cooling/test-cell/automation investment (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

**Leveraging AI**: Directly and structurally — Sanmina's growth engine now *is* AI hardware manufacturing (ZT's accelerated-compute racks), plus its 42Q software offers cloud-based manufacturing execution (10-K; excerpts).

**Management commitment**: High. Leadership has raised its FY27 target twice (from "double the business in three years" to "$16B+", now "more confident than ever") and describes itself as personally "very excited" about the trajectory (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md).

**Challenges**: (1) Customer concentration has risen sharply alongside this growth — top-10 customers were ~50-52% of sales historically but are now 67%, and the 10-Q flags a single customer at 10%+ of sales for the first time in the periods shown, directly tied to hyperscaler dependence (10-Q). (2) Component shortages (memory, custom ASICs) are constraining how much Sanmina can actually ship — management called this an ongoing constraint likely persisting into FY27 (SANM_earnings_2026Q2.md; SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). (3) ZT's revenue mix is lumpy quarter to quarter as legacy programs roll off before the next-gen program ramps in (Q4 guide for ZT revenue is *below* what was implied last quarter, purely from legacy-program timing) (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md). (4) Pace is partly out of Sanmina's hands — it depends on AMD's own "measured approach" to ramping customer manufacturing partners (SANM_earnings_2026Q3.md, analyst question referencing AMD commentary).
